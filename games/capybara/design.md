# Capybara: design

*The game's brief: what Ryan decided, what is proposed and what is open. Working title "capybara" until the game, the capybara and the seagull have names. Started 2026-10-21 from Ryan's brief and two rounds of questions; reviewed by Codex (GPT, via the Codex CLI) the same day, and its findings folded in.*

Ryan owns the goals, the lore and the art direction. A line marked **proposed** is an agent's suggestion that Ryan has not confirmed. A section marked **contract** is an implementation decision that code and tests hold to; the numbers it names live in the code (`feel.wrela`), not here. Everything else Ryan decided.

## The premise

A capybara wants a snack. A seagull keeps taking it. Each level ends with the seagull taking the snack away again: "your snack is in another castle".

The game is set in a zoo, as a day out. The easter eggs are cute, small and kind.

## The pillars

1. **Simple and functional, as Super Mario Bros. is,** with a few modern additions: the slide, swimming, and a hidden ride.
2. **Perfectly speedrunnable.** The simulation is deterministic, every run is a replay, and a run's time is a count of ticks.
3. **Easy to finish, hard to master.**
4. **A production game:** it ships on the console, with a cover and a release. The first flagship game: the only one in the production release (Ryan, 2026-10-30: Leapling and The Last Green were beta tests). Its cover (`cover.png`) and the console's hero (`hero.png`) are frames of the game, drawn by a still's build (`--set HERO=true`: the view nearer, the capybara in its right third, nothing over the scene) from a level's input script on the GPU; the console plays the theme's melody and bass from `theme.txt`, which a test holds to `music.wrela`.

## Shape

- **2D, with parallax:** play is in one plane, drawn flat and seen straight on, with layers behind (hills, clouds) that move slower than the camera. Ryan first asked for 2.5D (a 3D world on a fixed side view, as Ori), then chose 2D (2026-10-22): perspective skewed the edges a precision platformer is read by, and clean 2D forms suit the cast better than agent-built 3D creatures have so far.
- **The ground is curves,** not tiles: chains of points with rounded corners, so slopes are continuous.
- **Nine levels** (Ryan, 2026-10-30): an easy first level in the capybaras' own home, then the Wetlands (the first level that was built), then one level in each habitat round the loop below. The movement lab is a developer's alone (a build with `--set DEV=true`, where Tab steps through every course).
- **A map of the zoo, and a sense of place** (Ryan, 2026-10-22: "where are we in the zoo? each level should feel like you're in a habitat in the zoo and then the background should extend from there"). The zoo is a loop of habitats round a central lake, which the seagull leads the capybara round, from its own enclosure to the food court: (1) the Capybara Wetlands, (2) the Savanna, (3) the Rainforest House, (4) the Aviary, (5) the Reptile House, (6) the Polar Coast, (7) the Petting Farm, (8) the Food Court, the seagull's last "castle". Each level is in one habitat: its near layers are that habitat, its far ones what the map puts beyond it, so the next area shows ahead and the last behind. Ryan chose the loop and the Wetlands first from Claude's proposal; the order and the habitats' contents past the first are proposed.

## Movement

- **Run and jump.** One fairly fast speed with no run button: Ryan played a Mario-like preset (a run button, slow acceleration) and a Celeste-like one, and chose the second with a touch more momentum and float and a slightly higher full jump, then asked for a gentler start (2026-10-21). From rest the speed follows an eased curve: gentle at first, strongest a third of the way, easing into the top in about a third of a second. Holding jump while rising makes a jump higher.
- **Speed is the fun.** Ryan, after three plays: the game is most fun sliding and moving fast; hitting speed is the coolest part. The mechanics serve it.
- **The double jump:** one more jump in the air, refreshed on landing and on leaving water, with a flip.
- **The bunny hop** (Ryan, 2026-10-30: "that tech is kind of fun. We should keep it"): a jump on the tick of a landing keeps nearly all the speed (nothing slows the capybara in the air above its top speed, a tick on the ground costs almost nothing, and a fast landing on a downslope adds speed), so chained hops reach 30 to 42 m/s: a speedrunner's tech, as the dolphin kick is. The searched runs use it; the levels' checks time their retries at the running speed instead, as a player learning a level runs.
- **The slide.** Hold down while landing and the capybara slides. Its speed along the ground is its landing velocity projected onto the ground's tangent: land tangent to a downslope and it keeps all its speed, land flat-footed on a steep slope and it loses most of it. While it slides, gravity pulls it along the slope and flat ground takes its speed: slowly when it's fast (a slide off a ramp keeps its speed), more at running speeds, so a slide from a run ends in about a second and 6 m (Ryan, 2026-10-22: from running, it "lasts too long"). It can jump out of a slide and keeps the speed. Holding down on a slope also starts a slide; on flat ground it is a crouch.
- **Crests.** The capybara leaves the ground where the ground falls away faster than gravity would pull it down, so a fast run over a crest launches it along the tangent.
- **Water is speed.** Not a different way to play: the same movement, faster. In water the capybara runs along the bottom and moves through the water at a higher top speed, on a light gravity, and each jump press is a kick upward. Being wet is a power-up: any touch of water, a puddle too, keeps the higher speed for a while after. Up and down held in rhythm is the dolphin kick, past the wet speed: a speedrunner's tool (Ryan's idea, 2026-10-21). Swimming up through the surface is a breach: a regular jump out, with the double jump still to come, so a route chains water, air and slide (Ryan: no automatic leap; a breach is a regular jump's height). There is no breath limit (real capybaras hold their breath for minutes). (Ryan, 2026-10-21, after strokes and floating didn't work for him: "maybe water can be like a speed power up rather than different gameplay ... or physics works the same way underwater but the underwater sections are faster." Both are in.)
- **The camera** scrolls both ways. It looks a little ahead of the way the capybara faces, and further only past the running speed (a slide, a dolphin kick), so starting and stopping don't swing it. On the ground it moves up or down only when the capybara lands at a new height; while the capybara swims, or flies or falls past a band round the view's middle, it follows. **Proposed.**

### Ground and contact (contract)

- **The ground is exact lines and circular arcs.** A level authors chains of corner points, each with a rounding radius (0 for a sharp corner). The build turns each chain into line pieces and the arcs that round its corners. The simulation collides with these pieces exactly; the view draws them sampled. So there is no polyline whose segment density could change a contact, a tangent or a launch.
- **Closed chains are solid. Open chains are one-way ledges:** they hold the capybara only when its middle is on the ledge's upper side at the tick's start and it moves into the ledge.
- **The capybara's collider is a circle.** A contact's normal points from the nearest point of the ground to the circle's middle.
- **Ground or wall.** A contact whose normal is within the steepest walkable angle of straight up is ground; any other is wall or ceiling, which removes the velocity's component into it and nothing else.
- **On the ground, the capybara moves along its chain.** It is on one piece, at a distance along it, with a signed speed along the tangent. Each tick it advances by its speed, piece by piece, with no collision search for its own chain. Other chains still collide with it as walls and ceilings.
- **Leaving the ground** is decided from the pieces, with no positional tolerance, so it doesn't depend on the tick rate:
  - On a convex arc, the circle's middle moves on a circle of radius ρ (the arc's radius plus the collider's). It leaves when v²/ρ > g cos θ, where θ is the slope, with its velocity along the tangent.
  - At a sharp convex corner, and at the end of an open chain, it leaves at once, as off a ledge.
  - Where the contact's normal stops being walkable (up a quarter-pipe, or down a slope too steep to stand on), it leaves with its velocity.
- **In the air, the circle sweeps:** each tick is cut into substeps of at most half the collider's radius. A substep is short enough that the circle can't pass through a piece, so its contacts are the pieces it overlaps at the substep's end. They are resolved nearest first, at most four, deterministically by distance and then by the level's order: each pushes the circle out along its normal. A walkable contact the circle moves into is a landing; any other removes the velocity's component into it. If the circle still overlaps a piece after four, the substep's motion is undone and the velocity zeroed, so the circle never ends a tick inside the ground.
- **A corner point is never ground.** A circle that touches only a sharp corner (its middle outside both pieces' sides) is in the air and slides off it, as a ball off an edge.
- **Contact changes use one rule.** Landing, and passing a concave corner on the ground, set the speed along the ground to `dot(velocity before the contact, the new tangent)`. Its sign is the direction of travel along the tangent, so a backward landing slides backward, and no velocity is normalized, so a landing at almost no speed has almost no speed. If the new piece isn't walkable, the capybara leaves the ground with that velocity.

### Movement states (contract)

| State | Entered when | While in it | Left when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walk | landing without down held; leaving a slide or a crouch | input accelerates the speed along the ground up to the walk or run speed; speed above it decays to it; no input brakes it to rest; slopes pull it a little | the ground falls away (Air); a jump (Air); down held (Slide or Crouch) |
| Crouch | landing with down held, or down held on the ground, below the slide's least speed, on ground flatter than the slide's least slope | it stays; the collider doesn't change in the lab | down released (Walk); a jump (Air); the ground falling away (Air); the slope or speed reaching a slide's (Slide) |
| Slide | landing with down held at or above the slide's least speed; down held on the ground at or above the slide's least speed or least slope | gravity's component along the tangent changes the speed (an uphill slide slows and runs back down); a small friction takes speed towards rest, more at running speeds; input does not steer it; the speed is capped | down released (Walk, keeping the speed); a jump (Air); the ground falling away (Air); below the stopping speed on ground flatter than the least slope (Crouch) |
| Air | a jump; the ground falling away; leaving water | gravity (less while a jump is held and the capybara rises); air control; a press the double jump, once; down held tucks (the intent to slide on landing) | landing (Walk, Crouch or Slide); entering water (Swim) |
| Swim | the capybara's middle goes below a water surface, off the ground | below, under Water | leaving the water (Air); the bottom (Walk, Crouch or Slide, wet) |

- **A jump** adds a fixed world-up speed. Out of a slide it keeps all the slide's velocity along the ground, so a slide up a ramp launches; running, it keeps only the velocity's part across, so a running jump is the same height on any slope (Ryan, 2026-10-21: jumps up a slope came out higher and read as random). A jump counts a short time after the ground falls away (coyote time) and a press counts a short time before landing (the buffer), unless the double jump takes it first.
- **The lowest hop is any short press** (Ryan, 2026-10-22). A jump rises on the light gravity only while jump is held, so a quicker release is a lower hop, and the lowest took a press of one tick (8 ms), which the route search used and no person can press. Now a jump's first 6 ticks (50 ms, the quickest tap a person makes) rise on the fall's gravity, held or not: any press let go within them is that lowest hop. Held through them, the jump is given the speed that takes it exactly as high as a held jump went, and rises on the light gravity from there, so a held jump is unchanged. Every jump begins so: from the ground, coyote time, the double jump, a stomp's bounce with jump held, and a breach.
- **A double jump** sets the upward speed to its own (whatever the fall) and keeps the speed across. Every way out of the water gives it back.

### Water (contract)

- **Water is a rectangle with a flat surface,** set inside solid ground that makes its sides and bottom, or lying in a dip of the ground as a puddle. It is drawn as the ground beneath it shapes it, never as a box: a lake fills its rounded banks (Ryan, 2026-10-21: square water was weird, and it should be larger). The capybara is in water while its middle is inside the rectangle; it touches water while its circle overlaps it.
- **Wet:** touching water makes the capybara wet for a while (1.5 s after the last touch). For the first half the top speed is the wet speed (1.5 times the dry); over the second it eases back to the dry speed, and a faster capybara slows with it, never by a jolt.
- **In water, off the ground:** the air's movement, at the wet speed across, on a light gravity, sinking no faster than a sinking speed: a dive slows to it. Up or down held swims up or down instead, at one speed both ways, so swimming in turn up and down keeps its depth (Ryan, 2026-10-21). A jump press is a kick: the upward speed becomes at least the kick's, about a metre and a quarter of rise (Ryan: jumping underwater was "way too antigravity"). Kicks are a short time apart; a press too soon waits for its turn (the buffer, which lasts as long).
- **The dolphin kick:** each change between up and down held, at least a short time after the last, adds speed forward, the way the capybara faces, up to a most well past the wet speed. Past the wet speed, the water takes speed back, so it lasts only while kicked. Mashing faster than its rhythm adds nothing.
- **On the bottom:** the ground's movement, wet. A jump off the bottom is a kick, not a launch.
- **Leaving the water:** through the surface fast enough (upward at the breach's least speed or more), the capybara breaches: it leaves as a jump does, at the jump's launch, rising on the light gravity while jump or up is held, to a full jump's height (Ryan, 2026-10-21). Slower, it leaves as it moves, on ordinary gravity. Either way its double jump is kept.
- **A jump press is never lost at the surface** (Ryan, 2026-10-21): just under it (within a margin), a press jumps out at the launch instead of kicking; for a moment after leaving the water, a press is the breach's jump (a slow exit becomes a breach), or, after a breach, part of it, so it never spends the double jump. Leaving through a side or the bottom (where a level leaves one open) puts it in the air with its velocity.

## Rules

- **No lives.** Hurt or fallen out, the capybara goes back to its last checkpoint, the foes as they started, the clock running, with a moment in which it can't be hurt. R restarts the attempt.
- **The yuzu:** a yuzu on the capybara's head is the power-up that takes one hit, as capybaras in Japanese hot springs wear them.
- **No coins.** Each level hides a few snacks to find; finding them all is the 100% category.
- **Foes:** pests and grumps, not predators (proposed). Geese waddle the ground, pigeons fly a bobbing patrol, caimans patrol under water, and the seagull's crew (gulls) glide high and swoop down 3.6 m and up again every 2.4 s. From above while falling is a stomp: a goose, a pigeon, a gull or a Komodo dragon is knocked out, and the capybara bounces as high as a jump with jump held (a little less without), its double jump back, so stomps chain. Sliding into a goose bowls it over, which suits speed. A caiman's snout only bounces (a strong kick under water). Any other touch hurts.
- **Komodo dragons** look like the zoo's fiercest animals and are its sweetest. They are stomped on like enemies, but on one level (the Reptile House), one (`Kind::Ride`) gives the capybara a ride: touched, the capybara climbs on and it runs along the ground at 13 m/s until the ground ends; a jump press leaps off, as off a stomp with jump held. Built 2026-10-30; where it hides and where it runs to are the level's.

### Ice and updrafts (contract)

Ryan, 2026-10-30: the later levels use the moves we have in new, harder ways, with one or two new mechanics ("i liked your ice idea"). Proposed until he plays them.

- **Ice:** a course names icy stretches of ground (`Course::icy_from`). On the ground whose contact is in one, the ground's grip is `ice_grip` (18%) of its own: speeding up, turning, braking, slowing from above the top speed and a slide's friction. So a stop from a run slides about six times as far, a start takes over three times as long, and a turn slides on a while. The air and the water are as anywhere. Drawn as a glaze of ice over the ground, with no grass.
- **Updrafts:** boxes (`Course::draft`) in which the air lifts a capybara that is in the air: up to `draft_speed` (9 m/s) at `draft` (160 m/s², more than the fall's gravity, so one falling in rises within a metre). On the ground or in water, no lift. Drawn as pale wavering streaks of rising air.
- **Easter eggs** can be speedrun tricks, but not all of them.

## The levels

The zoo's loop, a level in each habitat (proposed past the first two; their layouts and backdrops are agents' work for Ryan to play and judge). Each level's course, signs and doc comment are in its module (`home.wrela`, `wetlands.wrela`, `savanna.wrela`...), its searched runs in `routes_<key>.wrela`.

1. **The capybaras' home** (Ryan, 2026-10-30, after his wife found the Wetlands too hard: dodging the creatures while still learning the moves). In the capybaras' own enclosure: every move taught as a lesson of its own, with nothing else to manage, a checkpoint after each, and ditches shallow enough to jump out of until a gentle last stretch puts the moves together (the first pit, the first goose, alone). About 220 m.
2. **The Capybara Wetlands**, the first level built (2026-10-22), unchanged. *About 330 m; a first clear in a minute or so, a speedrun in under 30 s.* The gates (a goose to stomp or jump; a ledge only a double jump reaches; pits of 3 and 4.5 m; a puddle in a dip and a hill), the big slide (steps under a pigeon, a yuzu, a long ramp into a puddled valley and a lip over a 12 m pit; a slide-jump clears it, one-way ledges in the pit are the slow way; geese on the landing to bowl over sliding), the lake (41 m across and 8.5 m deep, two caimans at two depths, a dolphin-kick line between them, a far wall 3 m above the water: out by a breach and a double jump), the gauntlet (mesas over pits, geese, pigeons, a puddle's speed for an 8 m gap, a climb that needs the double jump saved), and five snacks, each asking for one move done well. A recorded any% run (28.7 s) and 100% run (29.8 s) finish it without a death, and a recorded run crosses the big pit without sliding, by the stones. The autopilot's own first tries died where a player will (spending the double jump before the last climb), and the snacks were moved until each needed its move, not until each was easy.
3. **The Savanna:** speed: slopes and crests that launch, wide slide-jumps, a waterhole.
4. **The Rainforest House:** climbing: branches, trunks, rain puddles, a river.
5. **The Aviary:** air: updrafts, introduced alone, then over pits, then chained.
6. **The Reptile House:** water and the Komodo dragons, and the hidden ride.
7. **The Polar Coast:** ice, introduced alone, and the seagull's crew.
8. **The Petting Farm:** stomp chains, and every move again.
9. **The Food Court:** the finale, everything together; the seagull's last "castle", where the capybara finally gets its snack (Ryan, 2026-10-30).

Each level after the first is a little harder than the one before. A new foe or mechanic comes alone first, then is developed and twisted (Hayashida's four steps: docs/citations.md).

## Speedrunning

- **Categories:** any% and 100% for the whole game, and a time for each level.
- **Ghosts.** A ghost is a replay's inputs, run beside the player on the attempt's clock and drawn as a pale, see-through capybara. For a level: one ghost, chosen in the pause's options: the player's best any% run by default, the robot's (the route search's fastest), the human line's (its fastest within a person's inputs), or none. Built (`replay.wrela`'s `trail`, cast.wrela's `ghost`). The world's runs are ghosts too (2026-10-31, below, "Online play"): "The run above yours" (the place just above the account's on the course's any% board; the default for a player signed in with a place, until they choose a ghost) and "The world record", each from its replay fetched by hash and checked against it; and any run on the leaderboard raced with R or Select (below). After a run against a ghost, the goal's card names the stretch where the most time went ("Most time lost after the 2nd flag: 0.80 s"), from the clock's splits (`replay.wrela`'s `most_lost`). **Built; proposed until Ryan plays it.** Still to come: for a whole-game run, split times at each level instead of a ghost.
- **Runs and watching** (Ryan, 2026-10-22: "a view mode where you could watch any playthrough by clicking on it on the leaderboard"). Each finished attempt is a replay; the player's best five of each category are kept in their progress (below), checked again by replaying them when they load. The pause's Leaderboard opens them, on boards (Tab, a controller's shoulders or a click switches): the player's, and the tool-assisted runs (made by a program, never ranked with a person's: the robot, the route search with any input, and the human line, the route search within a person's inputs: no stretch under 3 ticks, and a slip of 1 or 2 ticks recoverable within a second), each any% and 100%, fastest first. Choosing one (the arrows and Enter, the d-pad and A, or a click) watches it: it plays back from its start (Space or A pauses, left and right seek five seconds, up and down change the speed from a quarter to four times, Home or Y starts again, R or Select races it; Esc, Backspace or B goes back to the menu), on to six seconds past the goal for the fireworks, under a bar like a video player's. Watching is the frames' alone: the sim stands paused with the runs open and takes only the keys that back out (`watch.wrela`). Built. The world's boards are two more (2026-10-30, `online.wrela`): the World, the course's any% and 100% from the console's server, each place an account's name and time, watched the same way (the bar names the account and its place); and the Whole game, each account's best on every level summed (a whole game played in one sitting comes later, with its category). On the player's own board (now "Yours"), U or a controller's Y posts the run with the focus, and the row says how it went. **Built; proposed until Ryan plays it.** Since 2026-10-31 (docs/plan-online-play.md, step 8) they are one leaderboard: the World first, then Yours and the tool-assisted runs as filters of it, then the Whole game; it opens on the World, or on Yours when the game was opened alone and has no world to show. **Proposed until Ryan plays it.** Since 2026-10-31 (the plan's steps 5 to 7): a board's replays are fetched by hash as its rows arrive, those of the rows shown and the three places above the player's, behind the player's own asks, and up to 24 are held, so choosing a row plays at once (one not held is fetched first). R or a controller's Select, on a row or watching, races that run: the level starts again on its title ("Racing Sir Wobblesnout the Unhurried") with the run as the ghost, and the watch bar has a Race button (Again moves to Home or Y). Search is a board after the World: F, a controller's X or the card's "Search names" opens it (a page of the sim's, `world.wrela`'s `FIND`, so typing never backs out or races), each part (title, name, epithet) completes from `platform/names.wrela`'s lists as it's typed, or is picked from them with left and right, and its results are the names' runs on the level, fastest first, each watchable and raceable. A result shows the account's runs on the level shown, since a race starts the level the leaderboard is for. **Built; proposed until Ryan plays it.**
- **Offline play stays offline** (Ryan, 2026-10-31: "i still want offline play to be fully offline and explicitly creating an account to be what makes things multiplayer interactive"). Playing never touches the network and needs no account: the player's runs, ghosts and bests stay on the device. A run made offline can be posted later, once there is an account: the replay is its proof. The game keeps the fingerprints of the runs it has uploaded apart from its progress (`capybara/uploaded`), so an uploaded run says so.

### Input (contract)

- **The sim ticks 120 times a second** on the engine's tick driver (`engine::run`), `@deterministic`, with the host's input events as each tick's records (language.md §16.2). The frame rate changes only what is drawn.
- **A tick's input is a pad:** the directions, jump and run held at the tick's end, and whether jump was pressed during the tick. A press and its release within one tick still count as a press, and two presses within one tick (8 ms) count as one. The sim turns records into pads itself, so the host's tick log replays it.
- **Opposite directions cancel.** A stick's axis counts as a direction at half its travel or more, on each axis apart, so the stick gives the same eight directions as the keys; the sim sees no analog values and a replay is bits. Keys and sticks held together combine (either holds a direction). Key repeats are ignored.
- **A replay** is the pads of the level's ticks, run-length coded, with the build that made it (sim and content), the level, the tick rate and the first state's hash. Built so far (`replay.wrela`): the pads, the course, the time, the snacks and the deaths, recorded by the ticker as an attempt is played (at most 1,024 stretches; a run past that isn't kept), and a board version (`BOARD`) in place of the build, changed by hand when play changes. The first state is the level's start, a constant of the build, so an offline run needs nothing from the server to start, and the server rebuilds it from the build to check it. Tests play the same records at different frame rates and check the same state hashes, and a tick log made in Chrome must replay on the native host (`wrela-host --replay`). Safari and Firefox get the same check when the project first tests them (vision.md: Chrome only so far).
- **No randomness in play** for now. If play ever needs it, it is a seeded stream in the sim, apart from the presentation's.

### Timing (contract)

- **A level's time** is a whole number of ticks: from the first tick the player has control (counted as 1) to the tick on which the capybara first reaches the goal, inclusive. No float clock. Every attempt starts from the level's start state.
- **A death** returns the capybara to its checkpoint; the clock keeps running. **A restart** is a new attempt: the clock and the attempt's found snacks reset. The saved game keeps best times and found snacks apart from the attempt, so a 100% run counts only what its attempt found.
- **Pause** is a recorded input; the level doesn't tick and the clock doesn't run while paused, so a replay's pads are only the level's ticks. The replay lists its pauses, each as the level tick it came after and how long it lasted in real time. A live stream sends a pause and a resume as chunks of their own, and its deadline doesn't run between them, up to a limit. Resuming has a short fixed delay. **Proposed.**
- **Returning to the console** ends the attempt; it isn't a run.
- **Progress** (`progress.wrela`) is the save the console keeps under the player's profile, which its session program (`session/`) lists, backs up, exports and imports: each level's best any% time and the most snacks one run found (the levels' map shows them), the best whole-game time, and the player's runs. A level's record is kept apart from its runs, so a new board drops the runs but not what was finished. The game stores it as it changes, so a closed tab loses nothing, and again as it returns to the console (F10, a controller's Home, or Select and Start held). The settings (the music, the clock, the ghost, the signs seen) are the device's, not the profile's.
- **A whole-game run** starts with a new game and plays the levels in order in one sitting; its time is the sum of its levels' times, restarts and deaths included, menus and pauses not. **Proposed.**
- Ticks the host drops when it falls behind, or while the page is hidden, are not run, so they never count.

### Releases and replays

- **Physics is locked at release.** In practice a board is tied to one build of the sim and its content: a replay names that build, and the server verifies it on that build. The verifier for each board (the game's build and the host that runs it) is kept for as long as the board is. A fix that changes play starts a new board version; old replays stay valid on their own board. Before release, boards can be reset.

## Accounts and leaderboards

- **Accounts belong to the console** (`platform/`), so every wrela game can use them. The server will run on Cloudflare (Ryan, 2026-10-31, after Fly.io was chosen on 2026-10-22): its protection (floods, firewall rules, rate limits, bot checks, a hidden server), one vendor with the website, and Durable Objects run a server build's wasm. Sign-in and the boards are plain HTTP requests under wrela.dev (`/api/`), so the session is a first-party `__Host-` cookie; WebSockets are for live play later. The server runs in the console's Worker on toolchain 0.13.0's Cloudflare host (a Durable Object with its SQLite store), on **Workers Paid** ($5 a month: Free's 10 ms of CPU an invocation can't verify a run; Ryan, 2026-10-31, after a survey of hosts).   - Sign-in and sign-up happen on one origin, the console's. The relying party ID is `wrela.dev`; the server accepts only the console's origin and no iframes. - **What anti-cheat can and can't do.** Each layer is evidence, not proof, except the first, which proves only that a run obeys the rules.
  1. **Re-simulation.** The server issues an attempt ID and the starting state, re-runs the replay on its board's build, and computes the time and the category's completion itself. A run whose outcome the rules can't produce is rejected. It does not show that a human played: legal inputs made by a tool pass.
  2. **Live runs.** Online, the pads stream to the server in numbered chunks, in tick order, and the server keeps each chunk as it arrives, unchanged. A chunk later than a deadline, a buffer past a limit, or more than a few pauses makes the run uploaded rather than live. This makes slowing a run down or editing it after it is played harder; it doesn't prove either didn't happen, and a precomputed or spliced stream played at real speed still passes.
  3. **Near the best known time.** A run within a few ticks of the route search's best known time is flagged for review. The search's time is not a lower bound, so a faster human run may only show the search's weakness.
  4. **A humanness score** on input timing flags runs; it never rejects them.
  5. **Video** for the top runs of each board: more evidence, not a guarantee.

  Build 1 and 2 with the leaderboards, 3 when the route search exists, 4 and 5 only if they are needed. Uploads are bounded (ticks per run, bytes, runs per account per hour), and verification runs in a queue with limits, since a replay is work an attacker chooses. **Layer 1 is built** (`boards.wrela`, `platform/server.wrela`): an upload's replay is at most 48 KiB and `MOST_TICKS`, an account uploads at most 10 runs an hour, at most 32 wait, and the server plays one at a time, 400 ticks a call (about 5 ms of a call's 50; docs/bench.md), from the level's start on its own build, and takes the time, the snacks and the deaths from the sim, refusing a run whose claim the rules can't make, a run of the lab, and a run that says a tool made it. Layer 2, live runs, comes later.
- **One board.** Live and uploaded runs are ranked together; the server records which a run was, and a run near the top must have been live or come with a video. Until live runs exist, every run is uploaded, and a board ranks each account's best alone. A board is one version of play (`replay::BOARD`): a new version starts new boards, and the old stay in the store.
- **A separate tool-assisted board,** which can include the route search's results. Today it is the game's own (the route search's runs); an upload that says a tool made it is refused, and the Terms of Service move such runs off the human board.

## The agent loop

The goal is that an agent can play the game, see it and check it without Ryan. Checks show behaviour, not good feel: Ryan's play of the lab judges the feel, and an agent tunes as far as measurement goes before that.

- **The sim steps on a pad** (above). Tests, proofs and searches drive the sim with sequences of pads; the game's ticks make the same pads from the host's events.
- **Proofs.** Each intended jump or route is a recorded run (pads and how many ticks each is held) that must reach its goal. A physics change that breaks one fails the suite.
- **Feel metrics** in metres and ticks, each with an accepted range: jump heights (held, tapped, double), gap reach, slide exit speed by landing angle, time to top speed. Edge cases get bounded regressions: crests of different radii at many speeds, corners, ledges, the water's surface.
- **Traces and stills:** the game exports the capybara's state for `wrela trace`, and `wrela-host <build> --frames n --input script.json --png out.png` draws any frame. The toolchain's `trace` and `bisect` don't run a program's ticker yet, so until they do, traces come from tests that step the sim. The lab's view has a GPU frame test on the hardware GPU; its frame time, and the sim's time per tick, are profiled apart from the checks, against vision.md's budgets.
- **The camera** has tests too: it keeps the capybara in view through the lab's pool and over its crests.
- **The levels' design, checked** (`games/capybara/route/design.wrela`), from each level's searched runs, so a course change that breaks a rule of good level design fails as a broken jump does. A move is taught before it's needed (a search with the move forbidden, the route tool's `--needs`, finds where), with no foe or deadly fall from its sign to there; the first foe of each kind comes alone; standing at a deadly gap's edge, its far side is on the screen (a property of the course: the searched runs' bunny hops fly blind by choice), and no foe is met before it's on the screen from 5.4 m (a reaction time at the wet speed); checkpoints are at most 25 s apart at the running speed (15 s on the first level) and safe to come back to; seeded random play never ends a tick inside the ground, past the course's ends or with a number that isn't one, and plays the same again; and each input of the human run has a timing window (how many ticks early or late it can be and the run still live a second on), a median of at least 4 ticks on the first level and 2 on any. Fun is judged by playing; these find what spoils it.
- **Route search** (`wrela tool route -- --level <key>`, `games/capybara/route`): a beam search over the sim's states, bounded in ticks, states and time, which treats two states as one only when they are equal (a hash match, then a comparison). From a segment's entry state (the level's start, or the tick the best run passes a checkpoint) it searches to the goal, a layer of a few ticks at a time: each kept state takes each of ten pads, and the best state of each cell (near in place and velocity, the same mode and finds) goes on, the best cells first, scored by the distance still to go through the snacks a 100% run must find. Deaths and passed-by snacks drop a state. It writes its fastest any% and 100% runs to `routes.wrela`, whose tests replay them, and starts from them the next time, so its times only fall. Its time is the best known, not a lower bound. With `--human` it finds human-possible runs too (`HUMAN_ANY`, `HUMAN_FULL`): every stretch of buttons held 3 ticks or more, jump pressed only after it was let go, and at least 95% of the changes of buttons, moved 1 or 2 ticks early or late, living through the second after (a player corrects a slip within it; with every later button fixed, a slip spoils a jump seconds later). It ranks them by time plus 30 ticks for each change that isn't firm. Human-possible means each input has slack and a slip is recoverable, not that a person was tested on it. Proving a jump possible is a replay; proving one impossible needs an exhaustive bounded search and is harder.
