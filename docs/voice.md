# How wrela talks

The words on wrela.dev, in the console and in its games. Written so a person reading them hears a
person. Ryan, 2026-10-31: the old copy "reads as ai slop writing".

## The voice

- **Short.** One idea a sentence. If a sentence needs a colon, a semicolon or a dash to hold it
  together, make it two sentences, or cut half of it.
- **Plain.** The word a friend would use. "Can't load your saves", not "The saves could not be
  read".
- **To you.** Second person, present tense. "You're signed in." "Your run is on the board."
- **Warm, not cute.** The capybara's world is a picture book, so the game can smile ("Sir
  Wobblesnout the Unhurried"). The console and the account pages are calm and clear. Neither
  gushes.
- **Specific.** Say the thing. "Your best: 1:23.4" beats "Great job on your run!"

## Buttons and links

- A button says what happens when you press it: "Post run", "Create account", "Sign in", "Keep
  this name". Never "Submit", "OK" or "Continue" when a verb that means something fits.
- Title case for nothing. Sentence case everywhere: "Create account", not "Create Account".
- A link reads as what it opens: "Terms", "Privacy Policy", "Lost your passkey?".

## Errors

Say what happened, then what to do. No blame, no jargon, no apology spiral.

- "Can't reach wrela.dev. Check your connection and try again."
- "That passkey belongs to another account. Sign in with it instead."

## Empty states

Say why it's empty and what fills it.

- "No runs yet. Finish a level and your best shows up here."
- "Nobody's on this board yet. Be first."

## Patterns to avoid

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| "A day at the zoo: nine habitats to run, swim and slide through." | "Run, swim and slide through nine habitats at the zoo." |
| "Up and down in turn: the dolphin kick" | "Press up and down in turn to dolphin kick" |
| "Reading saves" | "Loading your saves…" |
| "This deletes your passkeys' records, your sessions… at once." | "This deletes your account and everything in it. Your saves on this device stay." |
| "Connecting to wrela.dev..." | "Connecting…" |
| "The game library could not load. Check your connection and reload." | "Can't load the games. Check your connection and reload." |

And these, wherever they show up:

- A colon or dash joining two clauses, as above.
- Words that sound like a brochure: seamless, effortless, journey, unlock, elevate, delight,
  experience (as a noun for using something).
- "At once", "simply", "just", "please note", "in order to".
- Describing the thing instead of saying it: "This button allows you to post your run" → "Post
  run".
- Exclamation marks, except where the game is genuinely celebrating, and then one.
- Ellipses made of three periods. Use "…", and only for something that's happening.
