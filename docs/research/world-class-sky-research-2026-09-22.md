# A world-class sky on an ordinary GPU

Research date: 2026-09-22. This is a design and experiment plan, not a claim that the current sky has passed visual or performance acceptance. It follows the [implementation audit](sky-lighting-research-2026-09-22.md), the [cloud implementation and measurements](atmosphere-clouds-2026-09-22.md), and the final lookdev captures in `output/lookdev-snapshots/1790117683430-38033/source/output/browser-1790117724868-38212`.

Implementation follow-up: [measured sky-field experiments and shipped changes](sky-field-implementation-2026-09-22.md), including the rejected density cache, accepted lighting/aerial reuse, and honest before/after whole-frame costs.

## Judgment

Keep the current Hillaire-style clear-air atmosphere as the runtime foundation. The largest visible deficit is the cloudscape: broad structure is soft and repetitive, a fixed 32-step integration leaves grain at lit contours, and overcast/low-sun states do not yet make the terrain, cloud shadows, reflections, and exposure feel like one weather system. The atmosphere model also deserves a measured twilight comparison, but replacing it now would spend effort away from the dominant image problems.

The strongest performance opportunity is to avoid evaluating cloud detail and cloud lighting where no cloud can contribute, then reuse a small set of weather and lighting products across camera view, reflected sky, surfaces, and water. A semantic cloud field compiler could automate and validate those products. Empty-space skipping, SDF clouds, and amortized light grids are established production methods; Wrela's plausible contribution is compiling all of them consistently from one authored source, with conservative error guarantees and measured representation choices.

## What exists in Wrela

| Area | Current state | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear-air atmosphere | Compiled, composition-independent optical-depth basis; GPU diffuse, sky-view, and aerial products | Good scalable basis for moving sun/camera; finite scattering closure, table interpolation, and FP32 error still need image-level reference checks. |
| Clouds | One local 1.1–3.3 km horizontal slab, procedural warped noise in handwritten WGSL, 32 view samples, up to three solar samples per occupied view sample | Fast enough for an experiment, but no authored cloud types, regions, or deliberate large-scale silhouette. It is not a compiled `FieldDefinition`. |
| Reconstruction | 512×384 camera cloud product at the 1024×768 lookdev size; per-pixel fixed quadrature phase | Breaks up coherent contour bands but leaves static grain. Motion and disocclusion have no temporal reconstruction path. |
| Other views and lighting | Separate spherical cloud product for reflections/irradiance; surface shadow approximated from one density column | Cloud appearance, reflected cloud, and ground shadow can disagree in thickness and position. |
| Cache | Clear sky and cloud products have separate keys. Atmosphere table cache keys exact floating turbidity/fog values and holds four entries | Continuous authored weather can repeatedly cook/copy a small CPU table and invalidate GPU products; profile it before changing policy. |
| Authoring | Environment document has `analytic-sky` and scalar cloud cover, plus weather sequence controls | Artists can choose weather but cannot directly compose regional cloud masses, type, base/top, or storm fronts. |

The isolated production cloud-view kernel improved from **3.67 to 1.41 ms** for thin cloud and **4.72 to 1.67 ms** for overcast on the tested Apple Metal 3 path when compiled noise replaced analytic noise. Those timings omit other sky products, scene rendering, readback, and display. The final lookdev still failed artistic acceptance: reduced contour banding left visible grain, and zenith formations remained soft. Faster noise sampling is useful, but it has not solved the image.

## Lessons from primary research

1. **Atmosphere:** [Hillaire's 2020 real-time atmosphere method](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf) uses compact transmittance, multiple-scattering, sky-view, and aerial-perspective lookups to make dynamic time of day practical. Wrela has adopted the important structure. [Bruneton's tested implementation](https://github.com/ebruneton/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering) and [Wilkie et al.'s measured-data sky/attenuation model](https://cgg.mff.cuni.cz/publications/skymodel-2021/) are good clear-sky and post-sunset references, especially for a twilight comparison. Their models are not automatic replacements for Wrela's runtime architecture.
2. **Cloud art direction:** Guerrilla's [Nubis 2017](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-authoring-real-time-volumetric-cloudscapes-with-the-decima-engine) separated regional weather/coverage, cloud type, dimensional shape, and smaller procedural detail. Its practical cost reductions included coarse traversal through empty space, lighting only occupied samples, horizon/depth culling, and distance haze. The 2017 slide timings are PS4 examples from that scene, not transferable Wrela budgets.
3. **Close and fast clouds:** [Nubis Evolved](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-evolved) shows why one low-resolution, temporally reconstructed cloud renderer cannot serve every use. Near immersive clouds and fast VFX received different modeling/rendering treatment; Guerrilla reports detailed 1080p rendering without temporal upscaling for its newer close-cloud approach. Wrela should only pay for this if its game needs fly-through or close storm content.
4. **Sparse shape and shared light:** Guerrilla's [Nubis Cubed 2023 slides](https://d3d3g8mu99pzk9.cloudfront.net/AndrewSchneider/Nubis%20Cubed.pdf) combine authored dimensional profiles, detail noise, an SDF for empty-space steps, and a coarse sun-light grid. The talk reports about 40% lower cloud render time after decoupling most light integration, and 0.1–0.2 ms for its amortized light-grid update in its PS5 example. This is direct evidence for the approach, not a prediction for WebGPU or Wrela. Its custom BC1 SDF compression also depended on conservative decode error and the target hardware; portable WebGPU formats must be checked before adopting that representation.
5. **Sampling over time:** [Spatiotemporal blue noise for volumetric rendering](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~ravir/stbn.pdf) supports sampling patterns designed for temporal filtering. It does not make a 32-sample integral accurate by itself. A cloud history needs reprojected positions, wind-aware motion, depth/disocclusion rejection, and a fast-motion fallback. The alternative is higher current-frame sampling with a matching cost.
6. **Whole-scene agreement:** Rockstar's [RDR2 sky/atmosphere course abstract](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2019/index.htm) treats clouds, fog, ambient/reflection lighting, and weather as an integrated system. This matches the visible Wrela issue: beautiful isolated sky colors are insufficient when the ground, water, and horizon imply different weather.

## Recommended visual design

**Author the large shapes first.** Add a low-frequency weather map with regional cover and cloud type, then a vertical profile for cumulus, stratiform deck, and high thin cloud. The map can be compiled from semantic regions and fronts rather than stored as a hand-painted asset. Support distinct gaps, towers, anvil/sheared forms, and broken edge groups. Use existing procedural noise only to add billows and erosion to a chosen silhouette. Preserve enough stable world-space structure that a moving camera sees believable parallax rather than a repeating ceiling. Start with a local playable sky; a spherical shell becomes necessary only when world scale, flight, or altitude shots demand it.

**Make light tell the same story everywhere.** A cloud sample needs sun optical depth, diffuse sky contribution, and a controlled approximation to higher-order scattering. The same density and sun visibility should drive cloud self-shadow, ground/water shadow, and reflected appearance. The first lighting upgrade should replace the single-column surface shadow with a cheap spatial sun-transmission product. Do not increase scattering complexity until the shape and shadow mismatch are solved.

**Choose reconstruction by motion.** For distant, slow clouds, compare a stable low-resolution cloud depth/transmittance/radiance product with wind-aware temporal accumulation. For rapidly moving storms, high-speed camera turns, or near clouds, choose a better current-frame result or a separate quality path. Record the latency/ghosting cost, because a still image can hide it. Preserve scene-linear radiance through compositing; tune exposure after the atmosphere/cloud/terrain relationship is correct.

**Use atmospheric perspective at the actual cloud distance.** Blend distant cloud segments with clear-air transmission and inscattering instead of attaching an arbitrary colored layer. This should particularly improve the horizon, low sun, and overcast depth cues. Keep sky color, direct sun, aerial perspective, and material reflection on the same exposure and white-balance path.

## Where the field/compiler thesis can help

The existing geometry IR in `packages/compiler/src/ir.ts` already distinguishes a proven outside-distance bound from an unknown one. Cloud density is a different field: its warped noise and thresholding do not inherit a safe SDF step just because the underlying macro shape has one. The compiler opportunity is an explicit **cloud/weather field IR** that returns density plus conservative occupancy information.

Proposed authored node types: bounded cloud mass/envelope; regional weather front; cloud type/height profile; bounded warp/detail; boolean/smooth composition; and advection. A node may report `empty` for a cell only if an upper bound on all density contributions is below the visible-density threshold. Otherwise it reports unknown and the renderer takes small steps. The compiler can emit a coarse occupancy or distance hierarchy and separate cheap/full density kernels. This should be checked against dense reference sampling at composition seams and under wind/LOD changes; a missed cloud is a correctness failure, even if average image error is small.

From the same field, compile these dependency-tracked products:

| Product | Consumers | Rebuild condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coarse occupancy and cloud type | View march, reflection march, shadow queries | Shape/weather morphology or detail bound changes; pure rigid advection may use a coordinate offset. |
| Sun optical-depth / directional ambient grid | Cloud lighting, terrain and water shadows | Sun direction or cloud morphology changes; for a purely horizontally translated density field and fixed sun, the grid can translate in field coordinates. |
| Camera cloud radiance, transmittance, and depth | Main view and temporal reconstruction | Camera, sun/light, and moving cloud coordinates. |
| Spherical/distant cloud product | Reflections and diffuse environment | Weather/light changes, but not camera turn. |

The rigid-advection reuse statement follows from translation of the line integral: if `rho(p,t) = rho0(p - vt)` and the sun direction is fixed, then `tau(p,t) = tau0(p - vt)` for a translated domain. Morphing weather, vertical wind that changes layer boundaries, local lights, or a rotating sun break that simplification. It is a candidate optimization, not implemented behavior.

A second compiler opportunity is **footprint-aware cloud detail**. The current shader reduces high-frequency noise toward its mean as sample footprint grows. Because density is thresholded afterward, averaging noise can change cover and opacity. A compiled scale-space or density-distribution representation could preserve expected coverage while selecting only the octaves and texture detail visible at the current footprint. Test against supersampled reference images across altitude, FOV, and cloud type; do not infer success from noise-field RMS alone.

The genuinely interesting Wrela claim would be: *one semantic weather field can automatically produce a safe sparse renderer, matching shared lighting, and screen/compute-scale variants under explicit visual-error and frame-time gates*. Nubis already demonstrates SDF skipping and light precomputation separately. Wrela should treat automatic proof, consistent cross-product derivation, and representation selection as research hypotheses until a controlled implementation beats a hand-authored baseline.

## Ordered experiments and acceptance

1. **Establish the picture and the budget.** Capture fixed-exposure noon, low sun, twilight, broken cloud, overcast, and clearing from the playable scene. Include zenith, horizon, water reflection, terrain shadows, and a moving-camera/wind sequence. Save linear HDR and display images. Measure every sky pass plus total GPU frame time, CPU update time, bytes, cache hits, and p50/p95 on the current Apple GPU and at least one genuinely lower-tier target. State resolution and quality tier. The whole-frame comparison is the performance gate; isolated kernels remain diagnostic.
2. **Prove authored macro shape.** Prototype regional cover/type and a small palette of silhouettes with existing renderer machinery. Compare against current lookdev at matched resolution, time, and lighting. Accept only if artists can deliberately place recognizable cloud masses and the moving view retains parallax without visible repetition.
3. **Remove wasted work.** Add conservative empty-space traversal and occupied-sample lighting; then test depth/horizon culling where scene composition allows it. Instrument density queries, occupied samples, and false-empty rate. No false-empty cells are permitted in the supported field domain. Keep the current fixed-step path as a correctness control.
4. **Stabilize detail.** Compare current fixed phase, spatiotemporal sampling plus history, and a higher-quality current-frame path. Evaluate ghosting, variance, fine edge retention, and camera-cut recovery in video, not just stills. A temporal method must fall back gracefully for fast near-cloud motion.
5. **Unify cloud illumination.** Build a coarse sun-transmission product shared by clouds, terrain, water, and reflection, then compare its shadow position/strength against a high-sample reference. Check overcast contrast and low-sun silver lining after this change.
6. **Compile the cloud field only after the simple version is proven.** Compare handwritten and compiled variants at matched image error, authoring time, memory, p50/p95 whole-frame cost, and live-edit latency. Retain compiler complexity only if it makes the game or authoring loop meaningfully better.
7. **Audit clear-air twilight separately.** Compare fixed-exposure, cloud-free horizons and post-sunset colors with Bruneton/Wilkie references and a converged implementation. Investigate ozone, spectral approximation, ground bounce, and closure only for errors demonstrated in that comparison.

The immediate scope is a beautiful, consistent **playable local sky**. Planet-scale clouds, full fluid weather simulation, neural runtime scattering, and a large spectral runtime are not prerequisites; adopt them only when a game shot or measured image failure requires them.
