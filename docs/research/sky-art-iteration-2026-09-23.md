# Sky art iteration — September 23, 2026

This pass responds to the feedback that the previous sky improved in some ways and regressed in others. The main changes are larger connected billows, less granular cloud lighting, quieter background coverage, separate upper wisps, and cleaner distant sampling. The [comparison board](sky-reference-audit-2026-09-23/index.html) opens on the previous implementation versus this pass. Original audit images and all twelve external references remain available.

The source changes are in the production renderer and alpine environment. These are rendered volumes, not sky photographs or retouched captures. This is an art improvement with measurable costs and remaining gaps, not a claim that the AAA reference bar is fully met.

## What survived the visual experiments

- **A branching tower rather than a noisy column.** Connected ellipsoidal lobes form an uneven foot, shoulders, and split crown. Larger billows carry the shape; finer erosion contributes less to the interior lighting. The local light estimate uses the unclamped boundary instead of differences between saturated densities.
- **Background coverage controls weather groups.** Previously, reducing the background value still left large banks generated by independent weather offsets. Coverage now opens coherent regions. A density-only thinning experiment was rejected because it left tiny fragments along the horizon.
- **Preserved dissipating wisps.** A matched-settings comparison caught the growing-cloud boundary making the authored wisp family too thick. That family retains its thinner sheared and eroded field, with the improved light estimate. Separate bank and wisp integration cases now cover this distinction.
- **Separate upper wisps.** Seeded, oriented fall-streak envelopes replace the continuous rippled sheet. Their sizes and bends vary, and fine detail is filtered by the projected pixel footprint. Strong straight stripes and equally patterned feathers were rejected. The final upper layer is quieter, though it still falls short of natural cirrus variety.
- **Composition checked in the game.** The first revised tower looked dramatic in the sky study but crowded the valley camera. The final tower is centered at −8.2 km, +16.5 km, with a 1.1 km base and 5.2 km authored height. A lower, distant bank balances it. Background coverage is 0.31. The closer tower view on the board changes the camera and is labeled separately.
- **The evening gains remain.** Atmospheric transmission, twilight exposure, cloud shadows in the air, and the curved cloud shell from the previous implementation remain. A just-after-sunset view is included in addition to the unchanged golden-hour and blue-hour light states.

The same geometry was inspected from side, front and back light, three other compass directions, overhead, storm, golden hour, sunset and blue hour. The final scene was also captured in the alpine world. All captures retain their rendered colors.

## Sampling and animation

The quieter composition exposed horizontal sampling bands in distant procedural clouds. High quality now allows up to 1024 adaptive view samples at 1440 × 900. Balanced retains its existing 384-sample weather / 512-sample formation limits and 768 × 512 view, with small fixed spatial offsets to break coherent bands. The offsets do not change with time. Low quality is unchanged.

Changing background occupancy initially failed the existing motion error limit: keeping cloud boundaries for three frames produced a worst RMS of 0.007176. Background changes now expire history after one reuse. The corrected case is 0.003980 against the unchanged 0.004 gate. Stable camera motion retains the longer reuse period.

## Cost is still part of the quality gap

Complete-frame GPU timestamps, 60 samples per run at 1024 × 768 output on the available Apple / Metal adapter:

| Study | Median | p95 | Maximum |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Previous sky, matched surrounding source, Balanced moving camera | 17.27 ms | 30.34 ms | 44.83 ms |
| Revised sky, Balanced moving camera | 20.38 ms | 38.27 ms | 44.89 ms |
| Revised sky, High moving camera | 9.40 ms | 44.76 ms | 49.55 ms |
| Revised sky, Balanced advancing weather | 20.77 ms | 29.95 ms | 42.53 ms |

These are individual runs, not a statistical hardware survey. High and Balanced change more than cloud resolution; their medians should not be read as a controlled cloud-cost comparison. The High p95 shows the cost of its cleaner redraws. The matched Balanced comparison is slower in this run. The visual gain is not a free performance improvement, and it does not establish locked 60 fps.

The final static alpine world has GPU p95 **20.05–22.87 ms** and CPU p95 **8.3–8.8 ms**, missing its existing 12 ms / 4 ms targets. This includes unfinished scene content and is not a sky-only timing. Ordinary hardware beyond this adapter remains unverified.

## Remaining art limits

Some broad procedural banks and overhead storm masses remain too smooth. The upper wisps can still reveal their procedural construction; richer variation and softer feather breakup would help. Dense cloud lighting remains an approximation, and animated redraw cost needs further work. The surrounding terrain, vegetation and creature art also affect the impression of the sky, but were not changed by this pass.

The next quality decisions should continue to use the fixed-light comparisons and actual gameplay camera, not simply add fine noise or enlarge the main cloud. The more dramatic close study is useful for judging structure; it is not the default valley composition.
