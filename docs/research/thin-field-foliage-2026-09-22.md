# Thin field-authored objects: geometry, coverage and light transport

Research date: 2026-09-22. Scope: bounded code audit and primary-source research for needles, leaves, grass, hair and fabric. No new rendering experiment or performance result is claimed here. Implementation owners are independently testing vegetation representations and a bounded leaf material response.

The strongest next step is a filtered thin-primitive experiment, paired with a separately validated reflection/transmission response. Field authorship should preserve shape, thickness, orientation, material and growth semantics; the compiler should choose an explicit surface, analytic primitive or aggregate representation according to projected size and measured error. Rendering every tiny feature through a general field extractor is not necessary to preserve procedural authorship.

## What the existing evidence shows

I inspected both preserved plant-detail captures. The [fan representation](../../output/authoring-lookdev/1790110916847-18149/source/output/browser-1790110922147-18241/plant-detail.png) has conspicuous broad triangular sheets. The [thin-needle representation](../../output/lookdev-snapshots/1790111437792-20310/source/output/browser-1790111437899-20326/plant-detail.png) has much finer geometry but fragmented, granular lines and an almost bare-looking crown. These are development failures with different causes. They do not establish that the procedural plant source is inadequate or that triangles cannot render needles; they expose the need to measure projected coverage independently from shape and shading.

The vegetation owner reports that the pending wider/dense-needle revision has 49,765 needles and approximately 205,000 total triangles at review quality, with approximately 97,000 triangles interactively. Those are source-count reports, not accepted image quality or hardware cost. Widening fewer needles can recover visual mass, but does not generally preserve silhouette gaps, directional occlusion or lighting.

## Current implementation limits

| Area | Code evidence | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General fields | `packages/compiler/src/surface.ts` samples a uniform grid, with quality caps of 24/48/80 cells per axis, and extracts six tetrahedra per cell. It reports features below the sampling diagonal. | Thin solids can disappear before rasterization. Increasing an entire plant's grid to resolve millimetre needles has poor cost scaling. Local bounds/adaptive extraction or specialized products are needed. |
| Analytic rendering | `compiler/src/primitive.ts` recognizes complete static spheres/ellipsoids. `render-webgpu/src/primitive.wgsl` rasterizes conservative rectangles and intersects each MSAA sample. `realization.ts` rejects wind/deformation. | This is a useful pattern for a new thin-primitive product, but no analytic curve/blade rendering exists today. Analytic intersections alone still undersample features narrower than sample spacing. |
| Vegetation | `botanical-primitives.ts`, `botanical-mesh.ts` and `conifer-mesh.ts` emit explicit stems, folded leaves, triangular needles and distant fans. `vegetation.ts` selects a coarse product at a projected-diameter threshold, with no measured coverage-error bound. | Plant topology is procedural, while camera/shadow visibility remains ordinary opaque triangle coverage. Some general leaves duplicate back faces although the raster pipelines already use `cullMode: none`. Measure removing this redundant work separately. |
| Motion | `scene.wgsl` and `model/src/vegetation-motion.ts` share branch/leaf phase-amplitude motion and previous-frame evaluation. | Stable IDs and displacement bounds are useful inputs for temporal coverage. Wind moves positions without differentiating that deformation to update shading normals; moving highlights can disagree with the bent surface. |
| Visibility/filtering | Camera and sun pipelines use opaque depth and one/four sample rasterization. No general coverage material, alpha-to-coverage, stochastic opacity, deep shadow or directional canopy transmission product was found. | Noise filtering in albedo does not filter silhouettes. Temporal reconstruction cannot recover consistently missing geometry without suitable coverage samples and motion/history handling. |
| Materials | The inspected foliage response uses the skin family, wrapped diffuse, an added back-transmission term and a minimum transmission visibility of 0.25. `woven-material.wgsl` filters albedo coverage only; groom geometry is explicitly opaque tufts. | These are surface approximations, not leaf radiative transfer, transparent weave, or multi-fiber scattering. The materials owner is replacing the foliage response with an explicit bounded diffuse reflection/transmission split and removing the visibility floor. |

Three quantities must remain distinct: **geometric coverage** is the fraction of a pixel footprint occupied by material; **material transmission** is light passing/scattering through that material; **aggregate transport** accounts for interactions among many leaves or fibers. A coverage mask does not provide leaf translucency, and brightening shadowed foliage does not compute canopy multiple scattering.

## Applicable research and its limits

**Curves and blades.** PBRT represents narrow objects using cubic Bézier curves with width and flat, cylindrical or oriented ribbon variants. These suit different cross sections: a grass blade needs an oriented ribbon, while a cylindrical fiber needs a fiber frame. [PBRT curve implementation](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Shapes/Curves). For Wrela, retain authored centerline, width/thickness profiles, blade orientation and material/source identity, then test adaptive strips against bounded analytic proxies. A general Bézier intersection need not be closed-form; bounded subdivision is a legitimate implementation. Fragment cost and overlapping proxy area must be measured against triangles.

**Coverage-preserving sampling.** Hashed alpha testing substitutes a position-dependent threshold for a fixed threshold to retain minified opacity while controlling flicker; stochastic variants introduce noise. [Wyman and McGuire, 2017](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/rtr/publication/wyman2017hashed/). Alpha Distribution instead preprocesses alpha values so ordinary alpha testing behaves better under minification. [Yuksel, 2018](https://www.cemyuksel.com/research/alphadistribution/). Both operate on coverage inputs; neither creates coverage information for our existing opaque triangles. A compiler-generated procedural coverage atlas would remain a derived product, with its semantic source retained. For overlapping proxies, stochastic transparency offers fixed-memory, unsorted coverage sampling with correct blended colors in expectation under its assumptions, at the cost of noise. [Enderton et al.](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2011-08_stochastic-transparency). Camera and shadow estimators must agree statistically, and motion/disocclusion must be tested.

**Thin leaf response.** PROSPECT predicts spectral directional-hemispherical leaf reflectance/transmittance from structure and chemical constituents; it is a valuable source for plausible leaf optical coefficients, not a complete directional rendering BSDF. [Author's current model documentation](https://jbferet.gitlab.io/prospect/articles/prospect1.html). Start with front/back diffuse lobes sharing a per-channel energy budget, then add a separately budgeted cuticle specular response. A law such as `exp(-thickness / 0.005)` is an empirical attenuation scale until fitted to absorption/scattering data. Test isolated leaf transmission, external occluders and stacked leaves separately: an opaque shadow map cannot distinguish the current leaf's transmission from other occluders or colored light transmitted through them. PBRT's [layered material transport](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Light_Transport_II_Volume_Rendering/Scattering_from_Layered_Materials) is an appropriate offline reference approach. Its [thin clear dielectric](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Reflection_Models/Dielectric_BSDF) models smooth parallel interfaces, not the diffuse internal scattering of a biological leaf.

**Microflake aggregates.** SGGX represents directional projected area using a symmetric positive-definite matrix, supports interpolation/filtering, and supplies diffuse/specular microflake phase functions. It is explicitly designed for volume appearance and geometric LOD. [Heitz, Dupuy, Crassin and Dachsbacher, 2015](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2015-08_sggx-microflake-distribution). Its directional projected area is proportional to `sqrt(ωᵀSω)`. For a leaf cluster we can first measure projected area directly as `Σ Aᵢ |nᵢ·ω|` and fit the aggregate; simply storing an arbitrary normal covariance does not prove the correct projected area. Spatial density, albedo, visibility, and an actual transport integration are still required. A microflake distribution is not a replacement for a GI solver, and a coarse voxel cannot preserve a resolved leaf outline. Keep branches and near leaves explicit; test aggregates only when individual structure is unresolved.

**Fibers are not leaves.** Chiang et al. model energy-conserving hair/fur scattering with internal reflection and transmission paths within a fiber. [Disney's 2016 paper](https://www.disneyanimation.com/publications/a-practical-and-controllable-hair-and-fur-model-for-production-path-tracing/). That optical model differs from a two-sided diffuse leaf. Its internal fiber events also differ from repeated scattering between separate hairs. The transferable infrastructure is curve geometry, coverage, tangent/normal distributions, stable identities and aggregate LOD; material transport must remain specialized.

## Ranked experiments

These are proposed gates, not achieved results or claims of research novelty. Run each as a small isolated control before changing the world renderer.

| Priority | Bounded experiment | Measurements and rejection criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | One tapered needle and one curved blade, then a fixed tuft. Compare existing triangles with a small instanced proxy that computes filtered coverage. Test deterministic MSAA coverage before stable hashed coverage. | Sweep projected widths 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 pixels, distance, azimuth and wind. Compare integrated pixel coverage and silhouette against a converged supersampled reference. Proposed gate: ≤2% total projected-area error and no new persistent holes. Reject if noise/flicker or total GPU cost worsens at matched quality. |
| 2 | Separate thin-leaf diffuse reflection/transmission using actual curved leaves, a neutral reference light, full blockers and stacked leaves. | CPU/GPU parity, nonnegative lobes, hemisphere-integrated diffuse R+T ≤1 per channel, zero direct source behind a complete external blocker. Compare front/back/grazing images at fixed exposure. Budget specular separately before claiming full-BSDF conservation. |
| 3 | Derive a coverage product for one complete shoot from semantic needles; compare explicit needles, coverage proxy and current distant fan across the LOD boundary. | Camera and sun-direction coverage, shadow energy, mean radiance and temporal discontinuity. Proposed gate: ≤2% integrated coverage change and ≤5% mean-radiance change across the switch, subject to visual approval. Preserve seed/source identity and current/previous deformation. |
| 4 | Correct moving blade frames and test normal-distribution filtering rather than only filtered albedo. | Compare bent geometry normals and specular response with a dense reference during a wind cycle. Reject normal flips, grazing sparkle and shading that moves independently of the blade. Keep reflected-light integration correlated with coverage. |
| 5 | One static tuft as a small directional extinction/SGGX aggregate, compared to explicit geometry with identical leaf optics. | Directional transmission over held-out angles, multiple-bounce reference radiance, bytes, draw/fragment work and total GPU p95. Test sparse and clumped distributions; reject opaque-looking blobs or lost gaps. Introduce dynamic deformation only after static accuracy earns the representation. |

Priority 1 must actually create fractional coverage over a conservative raster footprint. Merely making an analytic needle hit test or enlarging a thin opaque needle cannot meet that requirement. A width-ratio opacity is only a restricted approximation; overlapping shapes, perspective, curvature and lighting/coverage correlation require explicit validation.

Record one/four sample rendering, with/without temporal reconstruction, static and moving cameras, wind, lighting direction, disocclusion and render-origin rebasing. Report CPU build/update cost, upload bytes, triangles/proxy count, memory, GPU median/p95 and capture resolution. Reference convergence, not a visually convenient comparison, defines numerical evidence. Preserve the same botanical source across controls so representation changes cannot silently change the plant.

## Compiler selection on a measured Pareto frontier

The compiler's useful advantage is knowing the authored structure before rendering: needle centerlines, bounded widths, branching, repeat distributions, material domains and motion envelopes. It can generate several realizations from one semantic source and attach their valid domains and evidence. A plant need not choose one representation globally: visible nearby shoots may remain explicit while sufficiently distant shoots use coverage or directional aggregates. Such mixing must preserve identity, motion and light transport at boundaries, and its batching/transition costs must be counted.

Use this finite candidate set first:

| Candidate | Existing contract fit | Expected trade to measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dense needle/blade mesh | `direct-mesh` reference/fallback | Explicit detail and normal variation; geometry/upload cost and subpixel raster sampling. |
| Adaptive strip/needle mesh | `parametric-mesh` with separate mesh payload | Fewer vertices at bounded shape error; coverage and light response still need evidence. This follows the same source-to-near/coarse-product pattern as the relief experiment. |
| Analytic tapered segment or bounded ribbon proxy | New explicit product kind, not a disguised `analytic-quadric` | Less geometry and authored profile preservation; more intersection/filtering work and proxy overlap. |
| Compiled coverage/depth/orientation shoot proxy | New explicit product kind with owned generated payload | Lower geometry cost with fractional occupancy; view-domain, overlap, motion and shadow errors. |
| Directional extinction/microflake shoot aggregate | New explicit volumetric product kind | Compact unresolved detail and transport statistics; integration cost, shape loss and clumping bias. |

`model/src/render-products.ts` already supplies `sourceKey`, versioned identities, dependencies, fallback, byte ownership, assumptions and distinct measured/real/numeric/unknown error evidence. `compiler/src/render-products.ts` validates serialization and dependency/fallback graphs. `render-webgpu/src/realization.ts` currently selects only direct meshes, parametric meshes and static quadrics, primarily using projected geometry error and matched cost observations. Thin/coverage/volume products therefore require explicit schema, payload accounting, renderer and selector support; the existing selector does not already provide this frontier.

Keep geometric error units separate from image statistics. In particular, do not pass a fractional-coverage error as the existing world-space silhouette bound and project it into pixels. Extend the metric vocabulary for coverage and shadow visibility, or keep those measurements in a versioned attached report until the selector can interpret them. Preserve the existing transmittance, linear-radiance and temporal distinctions. Tie every record to source/algorithm versions, camera and light domain, motion, resolution, sample count, reference convergence and adapter/browser/kernel.

For a fixed fixture and trajectory, let `C[p,t]` be fractional pixel occupancy and `Cref` its converged reference. Use these explicit quality coordinates:

- **Coverage:** `Σ|C−Cref| / max(ΣCref, ε)` and absolute occupied-area bias `|ΣC−ΣCref| / max(ΣCref, ε)`. The first catches redistributed coverage that the second would hide. Also report maximum absolute per-pixel error.
- **Silhouette:** symmetric boundary-distance p95 and maximum, in pixels, for resolved reference contours. Subpixel features without a stable contour remain governed by fractional coverage; thresholding alone must not hide their disappearance.
- **Transmittance:** RGB absolute RMS/maximum error over a documented grid of view/light directions and path lengths, including sparse/clumped and overlapping shoots. Measure transmission independently of display tone mapping.
- **Shadows:** visibility/transmission RMS and integrated blocked-light error on a neutral receiver, plus shadow-edge displacement in receiver pixels. Hold light size and shadow sampling fixed. Camera coverage success does not certify the shadow representation.
- **Temporal behavior:** after known-motion reprojection, measure RMS/max changes in the *error relative to the reference*, rather than penalizing true needle motion. Report disocclusion error and settling time separately, and include LOD switching and origin rebasing.
- **Appearance:** scene-linear radiance relative RMS with a declared denominator floor, absolute maxima, and fixed-display-transform crops under front/back/grazing light. Compare highlights and transmission color as well as total energy.

For each camera/light/motion/resolution domain and hardware profile, retain candidates that satisfy every required quality budget. Among them, candidate A dominates B only when A is no worse within measurement uncertainty on all chosen quality/cost coordinates and strictly improves at least one. Cost coordinates include GPU median/p95, CPU preparation/update, resident/transient bytes, upload traffic and cold build time. CPU and GPU durations overlap and must not simply be added as an end-to-end frame prediction. Verify any scheduling proxy with actual whole-frame measurements.

Run matched alternating trials, report variability, and do not prune indistinguishable candidates. Choose from the remaining frontier according to the game's frame/memory budgets; use hysteresis and include representation-switch/history-reset cost. A changed camera, shadow footprint, density, wind or hardware may change the winner. The result is an empirical frontier over tested candidates and domains, not a proof of globally optimal rendering. Outside validated domains, select an explicitly identified fallback and expose the reason.

## Broader use and actual GI/RT status

Grass uses oriented ribbons; hair, fur and fine wires favor curves; frayed cloth combines yarn curves with a weave aggregate; distant needles/leaves can share directional coverage/extinction products. Ropes, woven baskets, fences and perforated thin sheets can reuse parts of this pipeline. They should share product contracts and filtering machinery, not one universal scattering law.

The initial audit found sky-derived diffuse illumination, authored horizon/ground fill, shadow maps, narrowly eligible analytic finite-sun sphere queries, analytic quadric intersections and screen-space water transport. Atmospheric multiple scattering was separate from scene-surface interreflection; no scene-bounce solver was present at that baseline.

Subsequent implementation adds a [bounded static diffuse GI cache](../architecture/indirect-lighting.md) using compiled scene triangle BVH queries, irradiance probes and receiver-to-probe visibility. Its CPU/GPU, independent-reference and closed-box leakage evidence is retained in [the priorities review](aaa-priorities-2026-09-22.md). This is one diffuse geometry bounce with an authored sky surrogate. It excludes moving bodies, thin transmission, water and glass; canopy multiple scattering and general hardware ray tracing remain unimplemented.

Thin-field coverage, direct light and diffuse transmission have their own measured limits; passing those probes does not validate full canopy transport. The current occupancy and coarse-footprint experiments are recorded in [the vegetation implementation review](vegetation-authoring-2026-09-22.md).
