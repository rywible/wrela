# Botanical vegetation authoring

The [botanical growth and forest rendering implementation plan](botanical-growth-rendering-plan.md) defines the proposed next stages, performance budgets, visual gates, and species-growth validation. The implementation described below remains the current baseline.

Vegetation documents can opt into a semantic botanical generator through `botanical`. Documents without this field retain the previous conifer realization. Existing forests therefore do not silently change appearance or cost. Studio's **Enable botanical growth** action is undoable and routes through the ordinary document transaction and persistence path.

## Source and realization

`packages/model/src/vegetation-authoring.ts` owns the bounded schema, editable species presets, geometry identity source, and effective wind response. Pine, oak, birch, shrub, fern, and grass are starting points with different crown, frond, and blade realization. They are not botanical simulations or finished art assets.

`packages/compiler/src/botanical-structure.ts` creates stable, deterministic branch identities (`trunk`, `b0`, `b0/0`, …). Primary branches and up to two child generations expose angle, length ratio, taper, light-directed growth, asymmetry, and maturity. Child origins use the same two-segment centerline as rendered stems. Local branch length and bend edits move the attached descendants. Bare branch edits propagate through descendants; pruning removes a whole subtree without changing sibling randomness. Damage shortens and caps broken branches and terminates growth. Radius and clear-trunk pruning operate on the source structure.

`packages/compiler/src/botanical-mesh.ts` realizes the structure as capped tapered stem segments and folded foliage ribbons. Species-dependent scalar fields define tapered grass, fern, lobed oak and serrated birch outlines; the proxy carries curvature, normals, motion and source coordinates. Grass blades start at the root without wooden stalks. Fern leaflets alternate along the frond and taper toward its tip. Cluster density, count, leaf dimensions, droop, and deterministic leaf loss change actual geometry and canopy gaps. Bark and foliage retain separate materials. Per-vertex source identities preserve branch and leaf correspondence and remap correctly when cached products are shared by different documents.

Generic compilation is bounded at 768 branches and 16,000 leaves. Specialized conifers use at most 4,096 branches; their explicit triangle reference is additionally bounded at 60,000 needles. Both realizations additionally stop each mesh at 240,000 vertices, below the cooked mesh limit, including all proxy vertices. Exceeding a work budget emits `BOTANICAL_BUDGET`; it never silently claims full fidelity. Interactive and distant coverage realizations retain the same authored leaf populations and scalar fields. Distant geometry uses fewer stem sides and fewer ribbon curvature segments. Its projected-size switch is a heuristic, with unknown geometric error, as declared by the existing mesh detail contract.

The top-level vegetation compiler owns a 64 MiB bounded shared product cache and returns conservative union bounds for full and distant geometry. Geometry identity includes botanical shape controls and branch/leaf wind weights, but excludes inspection settings and stiffness. Live stiffness scales wind via the shared `vegetationWindResponse` function in both compiler and runtime instance updates.

`botanical-motion.ts` binds each vertex to its first woody limb and gives each leaf a stable phase and attachment-weighted flutter amplitude. Root weights remain zero; every descendant shares its main limb's phase. The GPU combines trunk sway, branch oscillation and faster leaf flutter with the current environment wind. All passes, including shadows and temporal history, use these same weights. `vegetation-motion.ts` provides the CPU reference used by Studio and picking; cooked products preserve the four-float vertex attribute. Full and distant bounds include both oscillation envelopes. This is bounded procedural motion, not a biomechanical inertia simulation.

Specialized conifers have a wandering continuous trunk, irregular whorls, sagging limbs and alternating curved woody shoots. The compiler derives a supersampled scalar needle field from authored needle count, dimensions, seed and damage. Three crossed curved ribbons follow each shoot. Near and far share the field; distance reduces ribbon curvature without replacing the needle silhouette with a broad fan. The alpine source uses 144 semantic needles per shoot at 8.5 cm length and 3.2 mm width. The explicit triangle realization remains available for reference comparisons. Trunk edits move branch origins onto the rendered centerline; limb edits orient attached shoots to the edited tangent. Zero canopy density and complete leaf loss remove all foliage. Generic hierarchy and leaf controls are hidden in Studio while specialized conifer controls are active.

## Filtered thin coverage

`ThinCoverage` is an optional compiled mesh product: source-local UVs, bounded power-of-two dimensions and a complete linear R8 area mip chain. No external bitmap asset is needed. Needle bases are perpendicular to their axes; all mip averages retain higher precision until final R8 quantization so sparse fields do not accumulate opacity drift. UVs, fields, motion and resource costs survive cooking, transfer and runtime residency. Object batching includes coverage identity, and resources are released with their owning objects. Texture uploads advance incrementally by rows or partial rows under the frame upload allowance; an unfinished field reports uploading, not a false memory-budget refusal.

Camera rendering resolves source-anchored stochastic occupancy in a cheap depth-only pass. One sample is used on the single-sample path; the explicit four-sample mode uses four independently hashed sample bits. The subsequent thin color pass uses an equal-depth test and no coverage discard, so hidden proxy samples can be rejected before the expensive lighting closure. It uses no alpha blending or transparent sorting. A hardware alpha-to-coverage experiment remains in the reference tool: it conserved a single layer but lost canopy mass with many overlapping ribbons, so it is not the production thin path. Directional shadows use the same filtered scalar field with a single-sample hashed mask. Camera and shadow sample patterns differ, while their semantic occupancy agrees. Thin proxies are excluded as conservative opaque visibility occluders. The unused MSAA motion attachment uses a supported 16-bit format; single-sample temporal history retains exact 32-bit identities.

These are bounded geometric-coverage approximations, not a volume scattering solution. Three crossed needle planes approximate shoot directionality; the nominal shoot-length mapping changes needle projection on unusually long or short shoots. Subpixel proxy rasterization, overlapping masks, stochastic sample noise and temporal wind stability still need acceptance in the target scene. Material transmission is independent of geometric coverage.

## Studio workflow

The botanical inspector provides species presets, maturity, hierarchy, canopy, damage, global pruning, explicit branch selection, length/bend/bare edits, subtree removal and restoration, stem material, and stiffness. The existing height, radius, primary branch count, seed, material, and variation controls remain authoritative. Selecting a species deliberately applies its editable growth preset; it preserves maturity and review settings.

The embedded plant/stand review projects compiled geometry and draws the compiled scalar masks, so empty parts of proxy rectangles remain empty. It supports near/gameplay/far distances, numerical camera distance, orbit, silhouette, stand size/spacing, and a wind-phase scrubber at five metres per second. A stand uses up to five deterministic source variants, configurable seed and age variation, irregular positions, rotation and scale. `createVegetationStand` returns installable world definitions and instances, so the same population can be reviewed in the production renderer. The canvas switches to compiled distant details beyond 25 metres for inexpensive geometry comparison. It uses illustrative colors and a perspective camera. The main WebGPU viewport is the authority for real materials, lighting, shadows, LOD selection, and animated playback. World population rules continue to provide persistent community placement, slope/elevation filtering, density, spacing, and local placement overrides.

Run `bun tools/vegetation-lookdev.ts` for four-sample production-renderer specimen beauty, silhouette, close detail, backlight, two wind times, and stand near/gameplay/far frames. `--small` reduces capture size and `--frame=plant-detail` selects a frame. `--heldout` reviews grass, fern and oak variants. `bun tools/thin-coverage-lookdev.ts` compares the shared coverage shader against a 16× per-axis supersampled triangle reference at nominal widths 0.1–4 pixels, including translated, oblique and eight-layer overlapping projections. Source documents and render measurements are saved beside images; successful capture means the frame rendered completely, not that it meets the visual bar.

## Validation and current limits

The botanical tests cover schema round trips and limits, distinct finite geometry across all six species, stable pruning, attachment coherence under branch edits, total leaf loss, damage, product identity, cache source remapping, distant detail reduction, and work budgets. Studio review tests verify distance projection, stand expansion, silhouette mode, anchored roots, and wind envelopes. Existing legacy vegetation tests still cover reproducibility, winding, and extreme wind transforms.

A generated six-species inspection image was reviewed during implementation. It confirms distinct plant structures and real canopy openings; it does **not** establish AAA visual acceptance. Current limitations:

- Branch sway and leaf flutter are implemented; physical branch inertia and torsion remain outside the procedural motion model.
- Species are procedural morphology presets, not botanical reference-matched species libraries. Bark fissures, buds, flowers, fruit, seasonal color progression, and tree growth over simulation time are not modeled.
- Inspection stands vary seed, age, orientation and scale of the current species. World-level biomes and population rules provide mixed-species communities; this panel does not run ecological competition or succession.
- Distant geometry is not an impostor system, does not cross-fade, and has no measured silhouette error guarantee.
- Broadleaf and needle shapes remain sampled scalar and geometric approximations. Appearance quality depends on authored materials and lighting; rendering and performance need acceptance at the target game's actual density and camera distances.

These limits are explicit to preserve a distinction between implemented authoring controls and finished, measured game content.
