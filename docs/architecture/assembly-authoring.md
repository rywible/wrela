# Dimensioned objects and machinery

Object documents can carry an optional `assembly`. The existing field stays in the source document so disabling an assembly can recover previous work. Assembly realization takes precedence while enabled. Its geometry compiles directly from dimensions, independently of field extraction resolution.

## Ownership

- `model/assembly.ts` owns schemas, semantic invariants and shared rigid transforms. Source units are metres and radians.
- `authoring/assembly.ts` owns editable beam/doorway creation, grid snapping and socket alignment.
- `compiler/assembly.ts` owns convex profile tessellation, swept surfaces, chamfered profile corners, repeated modules, per-part material groups, part provenance, wear coloration and clearance diagnostics. `assembly-clearance.ts` isolates triangle/box overlap and closed-volume containment tests.
- `runtime/assembly-motion.ts` partitions a compiled artifact into immutable part meshes once. Playback updates rigid transforms; it does not recompile meshes. The authoritative runtime clock drives joint motion and collision.
- `studio/assembly-authoring-panel.tsx` exposes part selection, profile vertices and dimensions, paths, repeats, hierarchy, sockets, materials, collision participation, wear and clearances. `assembly-joint-panel.tsx` owns joint, transmission and drive controls; small numeric/vector controls are shared by both. All edits pass through the existing document edit/undo path.

## Geometry and authoring

Profiles support rectangles, circles and strictly convex polygons. A path is an ordered polyline with transported profile frames. Profile bevels clip corners without reducing the profile's outside dimensions. Optional end bevels inset the cap polygon and add bounded transition rings, preserving the sweep envelope while giving front edges real highlight-bearing geometry. Neither is an arbitrary mesh edge bevel operator. Source validation rejects duplicate identities, nonexistent/cyclic parents, invalid convex profiles, zero-length path segments, direct path reversals, unordered joint limits and excessive realization work. Budget: at most 1024 modules and 80,000 triangles per assembly.

Parent transforms propagate rigid motion to child parts. Parents cannot themselves be repeated: repeated subassemblies should be expanded to explicit roots. Socket snapping aligns selected points while preserving the moving part's orientation, including rotated/articulated ancestors. Persistent fixed mates align complete socket frames and follow subsequent target geometry, parent and joint edits. Sweep endpoint/profile anchors keep sockets attached through dimensional changes. Newly created beams and doorway parts use anchored endpoint sockets by default, so length and direction edits preserve their assembly connections. Mate/parent dependencies are resolved recursively and cycles are rejected. A fixed-mated part cannot also own a joint; articulate a child part to avoid contradictory constraints. Detaching bakes the current rest pose. Grid snapping acts in the part's parent coordinate frame.

The doorway recipe preserves exact authored inside width and height and creates an explicit reserved clearance volume. Clearance validation first rejects disjoint module bounds, then checks actual triangles against the reserved box using separating axes. A closed-volume winding test detects a clearance entirely enclosed inside solid geometry. This removes diagonal/curved-module bounding-box false positives and permits exact tangency at the requested doorway dimensions. A one-micrometre tolerance absorbs roundoff. Diagnostics describe obstruction in the evaluated pose; they do not certify the full motion envelope or a player navigation path. Self-intersecting sweeps remain unsupported.

## Machinery playback and collision

A joint is a bounded hinge or slider with a local pivot/axis and authored initial value. Optional drives traverse the limits using a smooth periodic cosine schedule. A drive's `period` and normalized `phase` are sampled from absolute simulation time, so scrubbing and replay are deterministic. A joint may instead follow another part’s joint through a ratio and offset. This supports counter-rotating leaves, counterweights and rotational-to-linear transmissions; the result clamps to the receiving limits. Dependency order is resolved independently of the transform hierarchy, cycles and missing source joints are rejected, and linked joints cannot own competing drives. Source commands propagate through downstream links.

`RuntimeSession.setAssemblyJoint(instance, part, value)` records bounded external overrides for the next fixed tick; passing `null` restores authored playback. Multiple commands to one joint in one tick coalesce. Rendered transforms and collision targets consume the same command before physics steps. Checkpoints capture overrides, seeking restores them, and new input during replay branches the command history. The lower-level `AssemblyMotion.setJoint` remains available to standalone consumers.

Runtime saves use a versioned assembly entity state with the source identity, absolute tick and indexed joint values. The dedicated `assembly-state.ts` codec validates identity, limits, length and finite values before any runtime mutation. Indexed values remain within existing world-save field limits even at the 128-part budget. Loading restores motion and collision together, including worlds containing machinery without any characters.

The runtime creates a kinematic body per colliding part, with a box collider per repeated module. Decorative parts can opt out of collision while retaining their material, geometry and inherited motion. This preserves open spaces between separate parts/modules. Swept curved geometry uses conservative boxes and may need more narrowly segmented parts for precise interactions. Physical hinge constraints, force-driven machinery and exact moving triangle-mesh collision are not provided. Bodies obey the existing runtime body budget; installation rolls back if that budget is exceeded.

Part source IDs survive geometry splitting for picking. Existing mesh cooking carries positions, normals, colors and source IDs. Per-part material bindings are included in dependency validation. Runtime and compiler cache identities include the complete assembly source.

## Verification and limits

Compiler tests check exact dimensions, outward winding, profile chamfers, module repetition/material groups, actual obstruction, enclosed clearances, exact tangency, invalid topology, linked transmission order/overrides, joint clamping and absolute-time drives. Authoring tests verify doorway dimensions and socket alignment through a rotated articulated parent. Runtime tests check mesh reuse, decorative collision exclusion, linked override propagation, kinematic synchronization, host playback, fixed-tick coalescing, seek/replay branching, versioned save validation, and complete world-host save/load restoration.

This adds useful dimensioned assembly and machinery authoring. It does not establish finished AAA art quality. There is no CSG of assemblies, concave/hollow sweep profile, profile taper/twist, general closed-loop mechanical constraint solver, physically based edge damage, or generalized CAD sketch constraint system. Wear is deterministic vertex coloration, not surface erosion. Curved sweep self-intersections remain the author's responsibility. Visual acceptance requires inspecting authored assets at their intended gameplay distance and under production materials/lighting.

## Alpine gateway look development

`model/architecture-lookdev.ts` creates a human-scale limestone toll gate and broken flanking walls for the shared alpine scene. Its 99 parts include staggered jamb courses, irregular wedge-shaped voussoirs, a proud keystone, battered buttresses, damp footings and threshold slabs, angular rubble, nine individual oak planks, iron straps/rivets/pintles and a bronze latch. The gate leaf swings on a timed hinge and its hardware inherits that motion. Decorative hardware does not allocate physics bodies; the masonry, rubble, threshold and door do. The asset compiles to 10,000 triangles and has no structural clearance warnings.

WebGPU beauty, detail, clay and motion frames are captured by `bun tools/lookdev-snapshot.ts --study=architecture`; the source snapshot keeps the result stable during parallel authoring. The current beauty and detail frames show a clearer structural silhouette, readable stone/wood/iron separation and an unobstructed opening. They still read as clean stylized geometry at gameplay distance. In particular, stone faces lack convincing fractures, mortar and erosion; the threshold needs stronger contact history. This asset has **not** passed the AAA visual bar. The next art pass should address those observed failures in the material and geometry systems, then review in the composed scene at near, gameplay and landscape distances.
