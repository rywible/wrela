# Creature anatomy and performance authoring

The creature implementation already has semantic anatomy, persistent chart coordinates, bounded local sculpting, grooming, joint expressions, clothing, mounted components, source repair, candidate comparisons, rigging, and motion review. The coherence tools extend this system instead of introducing another character format or a parallel deformation pipeline.

## Source operations and organization

`packages/authoring/src/creature-coherence.ts` creates standard `CreatureOperation` values. Callers submit them through `AuthoringSession` to retain relationship validation, atomic failure, revisions, candidate isolation, undo, and redo. These helpers can also be used directly by agents.

- **Measured proportions:** `measureCreatureLandmarks` measures two landmarks in one region. `fitCreatureLandmarkSpan` scales the region uniformly to reach the requested distance, optionally including descendant anatomy and preserving explicitly protected regions. One scalar measurement deliberately specifies one uniform scale; it does not invent an anisotropic shape solution.
- **Clothing:** `authorCreatureGarmentLandmarks` places editable back-panel fitting corners. `authorCreatureGarment` creates a bilinear patch and two persistent anatomical anchors, with normalized cloth pins at the top corners. It rejects duplicate, degenerate, or folded corners. The resulting chart and cloth are existing runtime source, not a visualization-only preview.
- **Attachments:** `mountCreatureAttachment` attaches an existing leaf shape to a landmark, optionally bound rigidly to a selected joint. It rejects an already-owned component. Existing attachment realization maintains that shape's independent dimensions.
- **Expressions:** `authorCreatureExpression` inserts or replaces a joint offset inside an existing expression without deleting its other joint contributions. Existing deformation composes expression offsets with motion. Facial keyframes belong to performance authoring; these expressions are reusable static blends.
- **Review:** `authorCreatureReview` stores front, side, back and region close-up cameras plus an optional motion and its duration. Studio offers lit/silhouette views, motion playback and sampled pose navigation. The existing creature panel retains matched capture comparisons, candidate previews, sculpt/groom tools and numerical motion checks.

`apps/studio/src/creature-coherence-panel.tsx` exposes these operations through the shared domain-editor transaction and preview callbacks. The panel does not own a renderer or a separate runtime session.

## Live fitting landmarks

Garments now retain their four `fittingLandmarks`, and mounted components and garment pins retain an anchor `landmark` binding. A `creature.landmark` edit refits the dependent patch corners and anchor coordinates in the same transaction. Normalized cloth pin coordinates and chart revisions remain stable, so the existing compiler and simulated cloth receive the updated rest geometry without a separate fitting state. Studio uses this operation when moving landmarks.

Folds, collapsed patches and reversed chart orientation reject the transaction. Missing or cross-region references reject source validation; generic source edits cannot silently leave a stale fitting. Studio's **Keep current fit and release landmarks** action and `releaseCreatureGarmentFit` remove the fitting references while retaining exactly the current chart and pin positions. Region changes of bound landmarks require detaching the fittings. Proportion edits continue to scale the same landmarks, charts and anchors together, preserving their bindings. Source inspection exposes these dependencies to agents, and bindings survive save, undo and candidate adoption.

`creature-landmark-bindings.ts` owns propagation; `creature-fitting.ts` owns the shared patch orientation check; `creature-validation.ts` owns persisted-source consistency. This keeps authoring policy separate from rendering and physical simulation.

New mounts use `placement: "surface"`. The attachment compiler seats the actual primitive support at the requested anchor-plane clearance, including rotated primitive and field-parent transforms. This fixes the former helper behavior that put a component's center directly on the anchor and immediately failed its own clearance diagnostic. Existing center placements retain their behavior. An explicit attachment offset is applied after seating, so an author can still deliberately override the fit; **Fit component clearance** toggles this behavior in Studio. The check is against the anchor tangent plane and is not whole-body collision certification. Region-local anchors currently use the region's local +Y plane normal.

## Physical coherence during proportion edits

`creature-physical-coherence.ts` is called by the established `creature.proportion` implementation. Uniform scaling carries selected articulation radii, half-heights and joint-local offsets, pelvis displacement limits, anchor offsets/tolerances, mount offsets/clearances, cloth collision thickness, character-space contact ground offsets/tolerances, IK tolerances and secondary-chain collision radii. Existing code continues to transform charts, landmark positions, rig positions, motion/expression displacements, local sculpt details and chart-relative clothing correspondence.

Mass, stiffness, damping, gravity, timing, and material constants remain independently authored values. World-space contact targets and ground offsets remain external constraints. Mounted components retain their authored dimensions; their location and clearance follow the mounting anatomy. Groom strand dimensions and nonuniform surface mask support retain their established independent art direction semantics.

Nonuniform scaling of articulated collision bodies is rejected because the current runtime sphere/capsule representation cannot realize arbitrary affine scale. A secondary chain crossing edited and unedited anatomy also requires expanded edit scope. Errors occur inside the source transaction and leave accepted source unchanged.

Performance clips participate in the same edit: facial translation keys scale with selected joints, interaction alignment targets transform in model space, and selected contact ground heights and tolerances follow representable horizontal-plane transforms. An edit that would tilt a scalar ground-height plane is rejected. Alignment edits count as root-motion dependencies when enforcing protected anatomy, because their runtime correction can move sibling regions.

## Verification and limits

`packages/authoring/src/creature-coherence.test.ts` verifies measured fitting, collider and pelvis coherence, transaction rollback and undo, garment correspondence and pins through growth, rejected folded panels, mounted component ownership and dimensions, expression composition, and persisted review cameras. Existing proportion and creature-authoring tests cover rotated source frames, protected dependencies and unsupported shear.

`tools/creature-coherence.test.ts` exercises the complete garment path: author landmarks and cloth through source transactions, compile the character, simulate its cloth, translate the animated skin transforms, and verify that both authored pins follow the motion with finite simulated geometry.

The wardrobe lookdev builds and then refits a Reed Penitent cloak through the same transactions. Its capture manifest retains before/after character identities. Run `bun tools/character-lookdev.ts --fitting --revision=authoring-fitting` for clay, silhouette, face/expression, garment closeup, gameplay-distance and animated-cloth views. A successful hardware capture records evidence; it does not imply artistic acceptance. The earlier Alpine Sentinel beauty capture still shows visibly primitive anatomy and sparse groom, below the AAA target.

The first wardrobe capture exposed a real appearance-ownership bug: the shoulder's unmasked skin appearance replaced the garment's explicitly selected fabric. `groom-appearance-binding.ts` now leaves material-owned cloth charts and mounted component source surfaces out of regional skin/fur replacement. A compiler regression verifies their materials and vertex colors stay intact. The revised wardrobe fixture uses an independently authored ochre fabric, rear-facing key/fill lights, scoped fold and neckline sculpting, a bounded cloth lattice and gentle wind. It also removes the original study's rigid skirt placeholders where simulated cloth already exists, avoiding overlapping representations. These changes make the existing authoring capabilities visible for review; they do not establish a high-end tailoring or artistic-quality claim.

These tools are a coherent authoring workflow, not a claim of finished AAA characters. Clothing currently authors patch panels, not sewn multi-panel tailoring or a full wardrobe system. Expressions require authored joints and do not infer facial anatomy or generate blendshape topology. Scalar landmark fitting is not a general anatomical constraint solver. Review cameras derive from source bounds and need artistic framing for unusual silhouettes; lighting and actual rendered appearance still need visual review. Existing chart topology/revision constraints apply, and material/rig authoring remain necessary for a convincing result.
