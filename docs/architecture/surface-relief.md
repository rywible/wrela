# Physical surface relief

`SurfaceAppearance.relief` describes local-space bark grooves or stone recesses independently of the existing shader microdetail. Its authored amplitude, spacing, seed, grain direction, and target edge length generate actual near geometry. The original mesh remains the authoritative collision surface and the distant rendering candidate. An inward-only recipe intentionally preserves openings and the exterior collision envelope; it does not add protruding bark or stones outside that envelope.

## Compiler contract

`compileSurfaceRelief` refines source triangles conformingly, welding coincident positions across hard normal seams. Open boundaries remain pinned. Seeded relief displaces vertices inward, with curvature, local thickness, original bounds, and triangle-orientation constraints. Source normals, colors, wind attributes, material groups, and provenance are transferred to the new layout. The source mesh is never mutated. An optional material restriction refines only one material's region while keeping shared boundaries conforming; the runtime currently applies relief to single-material surfaces, including individual assembly parts.

The default compiler budget is 12,000 triangles per source; the hard limit is 80,000. Oversized sources retain their original geometry with diagnostics. Target spacing is intent, not a guarantee: the review reports achieved spacing, budget limitation, thickness/curvature constraints, pinned boundaries, and orientation attenuation. Refinement is not a substitute for sufficiently expressive authored large forms.

The compiler allocates one authored signal between geometry and appearance. Stone has broad noise, chip, and grit bands. Bark's original eighth-power rib wave is decomposed exactly into eight Fourier harmonics, grouped into broad, medium, and fine bands; flakes belong to the fine band. The DC component survives filtering. Geometry weights taper from one to zero over 0.2–0.65 cycles per achieved pattern edge. The same weights apply throughout the realization, preventing skinny source triangles from creating spatial filter boundaries. Bark refinement prioritizes transverse edges and includes slow axial warping in its sampling metric. Two-edge refinement chooses the shorter conforming diagonal, and realized shading normals use angle weighting.

`appearance` carries the recipe, geometry/residual weights, and nominal per-band tangent-slope variance. Residual depth is the complete authored field minus its geometric allocation; it is signed and is not extra outward geometry. Variance uses 64 deterministic procedural samples in a representative tangent frame. It supports stable unresolved-detail roughness estimation, not a certified BRDF or radiance error bound. Geometric safety attenuation at creases, thin walls, and boundaries remains distinct from this spectral allocation.

Generated meshes retain undisplaced `reliefCoordinates` and authored `reliefNormals` so appearance can evaluate the same source phase at near and coarse distances. These cost 24 additional CPU/cooked bytes per generated vertex and participate in transfers, cooked validation, product/cache accounting, and runtime memory limits. Ordinary GPU vertices remain 23 floats (92 bytes), including thin-surface UVs. Only relief meshes allocate a second immutable stream with that same stride for source position/normal attributes; ordinary meshes bind their existing vertex buffer in both slots. This saves 24 GPU bytes per ordinary vertex compared with universally embedding the six relief floats, while a relief vertex pays 92 bytes for its optional reference stream. Upload budgets and eviction/disposal include both streams. Reference normals preserve authored shading seams; displacement directions still come from welded geometric topology. The sampler and pattern helpers are exported for matched reference measurements rather than maintaining a separate test-only implementation.

`coarseDistanceBound` is a local Euclidean correspondence bound between the realized near mesh and its original coarse mesh. It is not a bound against a hypothetical ideal continuous relief field, a ray-depth bound, or a shading bound. Normal, radiance, temporal and numeric projection errors remain explicitly unknown where not established.

## Runtime selection and cache

`BrowserSceneHost` applies relief before material-range splitting. Static surfaces receive two compiled render products: the generated near mesh as the direct reference, and the original source mesh as a parametric coarse candidate. Their identities include the geometry, relief recipe, configured compilation triangle budget, and algorithm version. This keeps hosts using different near-mesh budgets from sharing a product identity. The near product's zero bound means equality to that same realized reference; ideal authoring fidelity remains unknown. The coarse product carries the compiler's Euclidean bound under the silhouette metric. The renderer applies its transform and conservative camera-projection bounds, then chooses admissible work using matched measurements when available or the existing geometry work estimate. Distant geometry can therefore return to the original triangle budget. Near/far candidates are not a complete image-error frontier.

Wind-deformed surfaces retain generated near geometry and an original-mesh detail candidate with a 96-pixel projected-diameter threshold. Existing generator details remain available. This path is explicitly a projected-size heuristic with `maxError: null`; rest-space relief bounds do not certify the animated surface. Skinned, dynamically deformed, water, mixed-material, and partial-draw-range surfaces retain their original geometry with explicit diagnostics. General mixed-material support is not inferred from the compiler's selective refinement option. Original analytic products, selected-product state, and opaque-visibility certificates are removed after replacing geometry.

The dedicated cache keys immutable source-mesh identity plus `contentKey(relief)`. Repeated instances and frames share the generated mesh. A recipe edit or new source mesh produces a new candidate; source buffers must not be mutated in place. The cache retains at most 64 entries and 32 MiB, counting shared binary buffers, source attribution, groups, and product metadata with the existing artifact-memory accounting. JS engine overhead is excluded. Original coarse buffers are counted once across cached entries and deduplicated against host artifacts in combined host accounting.

Entries already used in the current extraction are pinned. If the visible working set exceeds capacity, the original mesh remains usable; refusal metadata prevents cyclic eviction and repeated compilation on following frames. A later view can admit a previously refused source when unused entries can be evicted. Individually oversized results are weakly memoized. Reviews and diagnostics are capped at 256 entries per extraction, and cache disposal releases retained geometry and metadata.

`host.surfaceReliefReviews` exposes source, material, surface, realization, cache-hit state, and compiler review fields. `host.resourceUsage.reliefBytes` reports cache retention; cache, installed, and live usage include relief products. Collision remains the original authored shape, intentionally slightly outside inward recesses, rather than being rebuilt during render extraction.

## Verification

Runtime tests cover mesh reuse, recipe/source invalidation, memory accounting, byte-pressure pinning and refusal reuse, unsupported-source diagnostics, and preservation of wind detail candidates. Compiler tests cover complementary spectral allocation, golden samples of the original bark recipe, physical tangent gradients, exact undisplaced coordinates, bounded topology, and thin-wall separation. `bun tools/surface-relief-cpu.ts --revision=<name>` compares three unchanged recipes across three bounded mesh budgets at identical sample positions, against both the allocated geometric spectrum and the complete authored signal; omitted frequencies cannot masquerade as full geometric convergence. The host integration test verifies physical near geometry, original coarse selection under the projected geometric budget, matched measured-cost competition, material edits, and disposal. Hardware lookdev separately captures identical material recipes with and without physical relief, close and distant views, and lighting variants; passing geometry checks alone does not establish AAA appearance.

`bun tools/surface-relief-probe.ts` runs the production uniform packer and production WGSL in a compute probe against the compiler's source equations. Its 1,728 cases cover both families, signed seeds including both 32-bit endpoints, three physical scales, near/coarse/full geometric allocations, negative local positions, varied normals, and four pixel footprints. It compares band/DC values, signed residual depth, nominal slope variance, and complete/allocated depths, with explicit floating-point tolerances. It is separate from the rendered lookdev study: parity does not establish appearance quality, geometric error, or radiance conservation. CPU regressions verify exact seed-half packing, reference position/normal attributes, distant spectral allocation, and zero residual when all unresolved bands become subpixel.
