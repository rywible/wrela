# Charted forms

How to author a part of a creature as a charted form: a limb, a trunk, a sleeve. A charted form is drawn from its function at the creature's posed bones, with no mesh and no skin weights. Where there is a reference, you write the part's structure and a tool solves its numbers; where there is none, you write its shape as a rule ("A creature written from rules").

The code is `engine/chart.wrela` (the form and how it's posed and drawn), `studio/forms` (the tool that solves the numbers), `content/figure` (the human species' forms) and `content/hakuro` (what the hero is over them). `studio/judge` puts two builds before critics blind.

## The idea

- **A chain** is a line of joints: a shoulder, an elbow, a wrist. The form's path runs through them.
- **A place on the chart** is how far along the path (`f`, 0 to 1) and at what angle round it (`theta`, from the chain's `up`, toward `up × along`).
- **The radius** at a place is a sum of splines along the path times a Fourier series round it (`Radial<A, R>`: `A` control values along, `R` terms round, `R` odd). It is linear in its numbers, so fitting it to a reference is one exact solve (`chart::fit`): no starting guess, the same answer every run.
- **Posed**, the path is laid where the bones are (`Chain::posed`) and each vertex is worked out from its number (`place_of`, `Limb::side`). A bone's turn about its own length is spread along it (`Chain::spread`).
- **A character** is the species' numbers plus a difference (`Radial::plus`). A right limb is the left's, mirrored (`Radial::mirrored`, `Chain::mirrored`).
- **Drawn**, a program binds the engine's vertex (`chart::vertex`, over whatever it sees with: `chart::Seen`) and its own pixel, which reads its normal from the form (`chart::Vertex::normal_from`) and paints it.

## Authoring a part

1. **Write its chain** in `content/figure/chains.wrela`: its joints at rest, from the root's end to the tip, and `up` (angle 0). A chain can also be made from a body's rig where the tool runs (`studio/forms/second.wrela`'s `arm_chain_of`). Start the path a few centimetres inside the part it hangs from, as a first joint that part's bone carries, so the root stays under what covers it. Set `spread` below 1 for a bone whose skin turns with it near its joint (an upper arm, a thigh: about a third).
2. **Write its spec** in `studio/forms/main.wrela` (`Spec`): the file and constant its numbers go to, three lines saying what it is (`about`), the chain, the `span` of the path its reference covers, how far from the path the reference is read (`most`), and how far its root is tucked (`tuck`). List where the reference is before you choose them:

```bash
wrela tool forms -- --stations <name>
```

It prints, station by station along the whole path, where the path is, how many rays round it meet the reference and the least and most radius they find; then every third station's section, the radius each of 16 rays round it finds. Start the span where every ray meets it. Where the sections make no sense (nothing found to the sides of a collar), the path is in the wrong place: slice the reference and look before you fit.
3. **Give it a reference** (`studio/forms/targets.wrela`): `triangles_of` for a body's own triangles by part, or `Grid` for a sampled grid. The tool reads the reference along rays from the form's own path.
4. **Add its fit** beside the others in `main.wrela` (`fitted`, or `fitted_over` a base for a character's difference) and its line in `tool()`.
5. **Run the tool** and read what it prints:

```bash
wrela tool forms -- <name>
```

It writes the file and prints the miss: the root mean square, the size half are under, the size 95% are under and the worst, in millimetres across the side, measured between the places it was fitted at. Then the miss by place: a row a station along the span, a column a sector round from angle 0.

6. **Read the table before changing anything.** A bad row at the span's start or end means the span runs past the reference: shorten it. A bad column means the reference isn't reached that way (`most` too small; no effect once every ray meets it) or isn't there (an opening: the tool fills it from its neighbours). A miss spread evenly means the form is too small: more control values along for detail along the limb, more terms round for detail round it. A form's control values are spread over its whole path, not its span, so a shorter span adds no detail; a shorter path does (start the chain nearer the part). A joint where the radius changes fast (the back of a knee) is where a limb's miss gathers.
7. **Declare the part** where the creature is assembled (`figure::limbs::arm` is the pattern): an `engine::chart::Part` with its chain, its bones, its form, its joints' changes, its band, its folds and its caps.
8. **Add its test** in `studio/forms/tests.wrela`: the written file equals a fresh fit, and the miss is under what you state.

## A creature written from rules

A creature with no reference is written as rules: each part's radius is a function of its place (`chart::of`), about a chain of joints, on bones a cycle poses. `engine/tests/chart` (a hound), `content/crab`, `content/heron` and `content/serpent` are whole creatures written so, each in one file. Start from a copy of one.

Three fresh authors wrote the crab, the heron and the serpent from this doc and the hound, in 12 to 25 builds each. Three reviewers who weren't told what each was named all three and passed all three. What the authors had to find out for themselves:

- **Angles.** Angle 0 is the chain's `up`; a quarter turn on is `up × along`. For a chain running forward with `up` up, that is the creature's left, so a form's width is its radius at a quarter turn. On a flat limb, point `up` along the normal of the limb's plane.
- **Bones.** Bone `i` of a chain runs from joint `i` to joint `i + 1`, and `Part::bones` names one for each. `root` is what the chain hangs from. A rigid part (a head, a toe, a shell) is a chain of two joints that names one bone twice. A chain has eight joints at most.
- **Posing.** A bone's matrix carries a point of the creature at rest to where the pose has it. Build them as a hierarchy: each is its parent's times a turn about its own joint. Nothing plants a foot: a leg that must stay on the ground needs a two-bone reach written for it.
- **Where `f` runs.** `f` is of the path's whole length, its `before` and `after` included. Lengthening a chain's lead-in moves every rule along it.
- **Ends.** An end that shows takes a cap, and a cap closes toward the path's point; the rule shouldn't also go to nothing there. An end hidden in another part starts inside it and is narrower than it there, or it shows as a step.
- **How many terms.** A section much wider than it is deep, or a blade, needs more harmonics than a limb's three: a shell 3.7 times as wide as deep took five. Parts drawn in one loop share one size, so the hungriest part sets it.
- **Two tints on one form.** Paint by place: `chart::Vertex::place` brings how far along and the angle round to the pixel.
- **Views.** A thin creature seen head-on, and a long-legged one seen from its side, were the weakest views. Look from the side, the front and above, and at three moments of the cycle.

## Ends, and parts that don't bend

- **An end that shows closes** (`Part::caps`, `chart::closed`): over a stated share of the path from its start or before its end, the radius falls along a quarter circle to nothing, so the end is round and meets the side without a crease. A trunk closes at the fork of the legs, and where it ends inside a neck. An open end is a tube's cut rim, and a rim that stands out from what's next to it draws as an ink tick.
- **A rigid part is one form on a line its own bone carries whole.** Hakuro's head is charted about a straight line from under his chin to his crown, carried by his head's bone alone, and his neck is its own form through the neck's joints. Charted about the neck's joints, his jaw (which hangs below the head's joint) stayed with the neck when the head went back, and stood out from the throat as a spike.

## A hand

Each of Hakuro's hands is six forms (`content/hakuro/hands.wrela`): four fingers and a thumb, each on its own line from inside the palm through its three joints to its tip, and a palm on the forearm's and the hand's bones. No part of a hand is sampled: the sampled hands they were fitted to are gone, and `studio/forms/measured.wrela` keeps 208 of each piece's radii to measure the forms against.

- **A finger fits a chart well.** Read no further out than the gap to the finger beside it, each fits its sampled finger to 0.2 to 0.3 mm rms. Its path cuts a curled joint's corner over 8 mm, and its tip closes with a cap. It stands on its knuckle as it is: the crease there is the line a fist's knuckles are drawn with, and with a fillet a fist at a blow read as "a blunt, rounded lump".
- **A palm starts where the arm's own form ends,** not at the wrist, and ends at the knuckles' line. Hakuro's arm was fitted as far as inside his wrap, and past that its form holds a forearm's thickness: with the palm starting at the wrist, the cuff was thick and the hand read as a paddle stuck in it. Run on past the knuckles and read as far out as the thumb, the palm was a blob that hid his curled fingers.
- **A thumb owns its ball.** Its line starts back at the wrist, and its form is read only on the sides that face away from the hand (read all round, it took the palm's and the index finger's surfaces for its own). Left to the palm, which doesn't move with the thumb, the ball stood where the thumb was at rest.
- **A thumb grows from the palm** (`chart::vertex_onto`, `Grown`; next section). Two forms that only cross meet at a crease, and four critics of four called the crease at his thumb "a hard, pinched notch". Its root, which no reference was read for, starts on the palm's side and rises to its own over a fifth of its length, and its light leans to the palm's where the two lie together.
- **What's worn over the wrist runs well into the palm's end of it.** A wrap is drawn to a fraction of the arm's path, and the path is shorter where the elbow is bent, since it cuts the corner: at 0.86 the wrap stopped short of the palm in his guard and the sky showed between them, "a stray white crescent" three critics marked. At 0.91 it doesn't.
- **A fist drawn big** (`Limb::swell`) is its forms drawn thicker: bones that are scaled stretch a form's path and leave its thickness alone. A smear that draws finger bones out should leave the thumb with the hand, or the thumb's form comes away from the palm.
- **Judged** against the sampled hands, four critics a round on seven shots (four of the fighters' fists, three of his open hand at his hip, large). With a crease at the thumb: 4 votes to 17. With a fillet on every finger and the thumb held to the palm over half its length: 4 to 17 again; the hold had flattened the thumb's own fitted ball, and the fillets the knuckle lines. As it is now, over two rounds: 17 to 17, with 22 "no difference"; the open hand 13 to 9 for the charted one ("one smooth, believable form", where the sampled hand's cuff shows "a doubled inner band"), the fists 4 to 7.
- **What's left.** At a blow, the small fist beside the punch: three of four chose the sampled one, whose thumb lies flatter ("the thumb sticks out from the fist as a lump"). The open hand's fingers are smooth where the sampled hand had knuckle bumps, and two critics of four still prefer the bumps. A thumb that stands off a fist like a stalk in the recovery is the pose's, in both.

## A form that grows from another

`chart::vertex_onto` draws a form where it meets the form it grows from (`Grown`): a thumb at a palm, and whatever else is skin over two charts.

- **It needs the other form's side at a point** (`Limb::toward`): how far off it is and which way is out, from the radius and its slopes at the point's place on the other chart, with the other form's caps and swell. One reading is within a quarter of a millimetre up to a centimetre off a palm and as much as 12 mm out at three centimetres, so the vertex reads twice (`Limb::foot`).
- **A root that rises from the other side** (`rooted`): the form starts a fifth of a millimetre under the other form's side and rises to its own by a stated share of its length, slowest to leave. Hold it only over what was never fitted. A thumb held over half its length lost the ball the fit had given it.
- **A fillet by the angle the sides meet at** (`chart::fillet`): the arc that touches both. Sides at right angles are moved the fillet's radius where they cross; sides that meet nearly flush hardly at all (the radius times 1 − √(1 − tan²(φ/2)), φ the angle between their normals). A fillet by distance alone pushed a thumb's ball, which lies along the palm, the whole radius out of it as a ridge.
- **Light that leans to the other side's** (`Grown::leant`): within the fillet's radius of the other form the normal is the other form's. Two sides that meet nearly flush have next to no fillet between them and are still lit as two; this, not the geometry, was what removed the seam. Both forms' pixels must read the same side for it: his hands' pieces read their normals from their whole forms (`hakuro::shaded_hand`), not softened ones.
- **A piece laid on another** (`Grown::laid`, `Grown::cover`): within a stated distance either side of the other form's side, its side is put on that side, and only where the other form's cover has it (a jacket's shoulder on its body; "Garments").
- **A vertex that nothing can move does none of this.** Where the other form isn't, or the point is out of reach of the hold, the laying and the fillet, the vertex is `chart::vertex`'s for one place found on the other form's chart (`Limb::toward_at`). Reading the other form's side for every vertex of a jacket's collar and shoulders cost the combat lab about a millisecond a frame; skipped where it can't matter, about a fifth of one (the best of six runs each, on a busy machine).
- **Find the cause in numbers.** The seam outlasted three changes to the geometry. A test that printed the thumb's side against the palm's, place by place, showed half the readings were wrong: a rounding difference had been taken for "past the path's end".
- **A head grows from its neck too.** His head is filleted onto his neck (`grows_from`, `grown`), so jaw and nape run into it.
- **Don't keep a form that was fitted to what a body wore.** Hakuro's neck and head were read off the body he was sampled from, which wore a high collar and a wrap round its head. The neck's form was the collar's girth, 17 to 19 cm through, with its folds in it; the head's, below his cheekbones, was the wrap's bulk, as wide as his skull down to his chin; and past the root of his neck his trunk's form followed nothing, and swelled. Under the collar nobody saw any of it. With his neck bare, his neck was as wide as his head and lumpy, the back of his head hung over it as a ball, his trunk's top stood out of his throat when his head went back, and no mask sat right on that head. Each is now written as what it is and put in a form's numbers with `chart::of`, once, as a constant: his neck (`hakuro::neck_radius`), his head (`hakuro::head_radius`, read off his skull), and his trunk's top trimmed to a stub inside his neck (`hakuro::trunk_trim`).
- **Model the skull, and build the head on it.** `shapes::skull` is one field: the vault his hair already grew from (measured), a base behind his jaw, his cheekbones and upper jaw, and a lower jaw with a chin, cut to a jaw line that rises from his chin to its corners. Widest over his ears, then his cheekbones, then his jaw's corners, then his chin; in thirds from his chin to his hairline (the foot of his nose, his brow). Everything on his head is made from it: his head's wrap is its surface (`head_radius` finds it from outside in, since a chin overhangs the line a head is charted about), his hair grows from its vault (`shapes::cranium`), and his mask is its face a centimetre out (`shapes::fox`, `MASK_OFF`) with a muzzle, brows, ears and cheek fur added. A mask made of its own ellipsoid was first 2 to 3 cm wider than his head a side, a faceplate held in front of him, and then his head's width but no longer following his jaw. The mask's inside and its rim's cut are painted dark, and so is the inside of each eye's slit: lacquered like its face, they showed white from behind and aslant.
- **A neck tapers, and little of it shows.** A column that widened only at its foot, inside his collar, showed over the collar as a straight pipe 10 cm long, and read as too long. His neck widens the whole way down from his jaw (12 cm across) into the slope of his shoulders (17 at his collar's top); his head's wrap comes down his nape as a hairline does (`hakuro::nape`); and his collar stands a centimetre and a half higher behind. What shows of his nape is 4 cm.

## What changes with the pose

A form made again along its posed path already bends. How the path takes a bent joint matters: a limb's chain cuts the joint's corner (`Chain::cut` 1), following the arc that meets both bones where the bend's rounding starts and ends. Read about that path, the reference body posed is nearer itself at rest than about a path through the joint (10.1 mm rms left against 12.1), and a deep fold reads as one arm, not two lobes. A longer rounding than 5 cm fitted worse.

Three things are added on top, all zero at rest:

- **A joint's change** (`chart::Change`, `Radial::bent`): two forms written for a bend toward angle 0, one times the bend's angle and one times its square, turned to where the bend is. The tool solves them against the reference body posed by its own skin weights, over poses of captured motion (`studio/forms/joints.wrela`). A change holds at the deepest bend it was solved over (`upto`), since its square would carry it past anything it was solved against. Each joint's change is kept to its own stretch of the path, as far as halfway to the joints either side: left free, the solve trades one joint's change against the next's where the poses bend both, and a straight arm hanging at the side had its forearm thinned 14 mm by its shoulder's change.
- **A solved change is only as good as the reference's skin weights.** At a shoulder they collapse the cap, so the arm takes half of what was solved there (`figure::limbs::SHOULDER_TAKEN`). Judge a change in the poses the solve saw least: a limb hanging straight, a limb raised.
- **Named changes** (`content/figure/flex.wrela`): what a muscle does, as a bump on the chart in the bend's own frame, and the point of an elbow on the outside of its bend (the path cuts a bent joint's corner, so without it a slight bend reads as a hose's). These are an author's estimates; say so where you write one, and keep them small: a bump twice as high read as a lump, a point twice as high as a kink.
- **A root that a deep bend swings into sight keeps its width.** A leg's root was narrowed inside the hip (`tuck` 0.6), and in a crouch or a roll the hip's fold showed the narrowed stretch: critics called the thigh thin where it left the trunk. More bulk at the seat as a named change, and less of the hip's solved change, were each tried and neither changed the picture. The leg's root now keeps its whole width (`tuck` 1) and is drawn 6 cm under the trunk, which is itself under what's worn: nearer than the trunk, the full root showed as a patch at the belt.
- **A fold** (`chart::Fold`): inside a hinge's bend, the radius is limited to the room to the plane that halves the bend, so the limb's two lengths meet at a crease. Name the hinge in the part's `folds`.

### A hinge's point and its crease

A chain's path cuts a bent joint's corner, so a form about it bends as a hose does. Bent an angle φ, the path passes `round · tan(φ / 4)` inside the joint: 13 mm at a radian, 26 mm at the 110° of a runner's arm. What a bone gives the outside of the bend has to be put back as a named change (`figure::flex`'s `ELBOW_POINT`, three quarters of what the cut takes); at a fifth of that, a runner's elbow read as "pinched off". And on the inside, the two lengths press into each other (`chart::GIVE`, 24 mm): at half that the crease was a sharp notch, as a hose kinks.

## Exaggeration, tried and taken out

A named change follows a joint's angle. A drawing could also follow the move: muscles that stand at a blow, by the move's own timing. This was tried on the combat lab's cross and did not earn its place.

- **What was tried.** The striker's arms tensed (a form added to each arm's: the shoulder's cap, the triceps, two forearm masses), by how fast the fist was going in and held while it was on the face. Judged against the plain cross, one constant apart (`wrela tool judge`'s `--base-set`), on the cross's fastest drawings (`--shots strike`).
- **What critics said.** At 5 mm the change could not be found in the pictures. At 10 mm four critics gave 3 votes each way and 10 for no difference, and where they saw it they called it "a small lump in the contour" as often as a clearer elbow. A forearm thickening toward the fist frayed the arm's outline and was not put to critics.
- **Why, as I read it.** The cross's fastest drawings are near silhouettes, and the punching arm is in a black sleeve: there is no shading inside it for a muscle to show in, only an outline, and the ink reads small outline changes as roughness. The fist drawn big and the head squashed, which the lab already does, are changes of a size the style shows.
- **What it leaves.** `--shots strike` (the going-in, contact, landed and held drawings) for judging a strike. Critics judged stills; whether an exaggeration reads in motion is a question for a person watching at full rate.

## Shading

A pixel reads its normal from the chart itself (`chart::normal_at`): its place on the chart and the section's plane come down from the vertices, and the radius's slopes there give the normal. It is as exact on a coarse grid as on a fine one, which a mesh's normals are not. Read it from the posed form with its finest harmonics damped (`Radial::softened`): a fit's ripples are a fraction of a millimetre on the silhouette, but they tilt the surface enough to fray a cel's bands of light. Where a folded hinge holds the side in, or inside a cap, use the vertex's normal (`Limb::folded`). The damped harmonics can be dropped outright (`Radial::kept`): the pixel then sums fewer terms for the same light, within a tenth of a millimetre of radius.

## Garments

- **A tight garment** is a difference over the body's form on the same chain (`fitted_over`). One with an edge of its own (a wrap round a forearm, a gauntlet) is a second part on the arm's chain and bones, its form the arm's plus a little, drawn over its own band. Stiff leather stands 3.5 mm off, and the ink draws its edge as one line. Wound cloth sits 1.5 mm off, under what the ink takes for an edge: at 2.5 mm the ink broke its edge into dashes, and at 3.5 mm it read as a boxy cuff.
- **A loose garment about the same bones** is its own form on the body's chain (the jacket on the trunk's), so it goes where the body goes.
- **A body under a garment** is drawn under the garment the whole way (`Part::sunk` reaching past its end), closed at both ends, and on a coarser grid (`Part::coarser`): it shows no outline of its own. Hakuro's trunk under his jacket is drawn 4 cm under and two levels coarser. The 4 cm was set when his jacket was a skinned mesh, which didn't bend as his charted trunk did; less hasn't been tried since.
- **A structured garment is a form for each run of its surface.** Hakuro's jacket is a boxy vest: about his spine, the radius jumps 14 cm across his shoulders' flat tops, and one form missed his collar by 19 mm. As four it fits: its body about his spine up to under its shoulders, its collar about a line straight up behind his neck, and each shoulder about a line along his collarbone and on down his upper arm.
- **Each form is fitted only to what's its own of the garment,** and 3 cm round that. Fitted to every ray it met, the body's form about his spine tried to follow shoulders 25 cm out and stood 15 to 75 mm off the jacket's back beside them; fitted to its own region it's within 2.1 mm rms there, and its worst fell from 63 mm to 27.
- **A form needs something past its end to be fitted to.** The body's last centimetres under the collar had nothing above them and stood off the back as a dark band. The collar's line now starts 7 cm under the collar's foot, so the collar's form carries the top of the back, and the body stops under it.
- **Pieces that overlap are laid on one another** (`chart::vertex_onto`, `Grown::laid`): a piece's side within 12 mm of the body's, where the body's cover has the body, is put on the body's side and lit as it. The body's cover runs 3 cm on under the pieces that lie on it. Each as its own sheet, a shoulder stood off the body as a plate with a shade of its own ("blotchy dark patches on the upper back").
- **What's worn over a shoulder rides the arm.** Charted about a line his chest carries, the jacket's shoulders stood out as flat plates while his arms hung. Their line bends at his shoulder's joint, as his arm's does, with his upper arm's turn spread to his elbow so the shoulder takes little of it.
- **Only some of what's worn over a joint rides the far bone** (`chart::Stay`, `Part::stays`). A form goes where its path goes, whole, and a jacket's shoulder doesn't: its top rides the arm, and where its edge runs into the body's at the armhole it lies on the chest. So each place on a part's chart has a share that stays with another bone: by the angle round the path (none within `round.y` of an angle, all from `round.z` off it) and along it (none before `along.x`, all from `along.y`). A place's point is that share of the way from where the path has it to where the bone has the same place of the form at rest: a skin weight, written over a chart. Hakuro's shoulders stay on his chest (bone 3) from 1.1 to 1.4 radians off straight up: the cap goes over his arm wherever it is, and the armhole's binding is one loop however his arm swings. With none of it staying, the cap's ends came away from the armhole in his run and the gold line broke; with more of it staying, the cap stood out from his hanging arm as a wing.
- **A piece that carries a surface on from where another form stops is lit as that form** (`Grown::beyond`) for so many places past the edge of that form's cover. The collar's foot carries the top of his back up from where the jacket's body ends; lit by the collar's own form it showed from behind as a panel set into his back. At 14 places the whole collar took the back's light and lost its own roundness; it's 10.
- **An edge is a rule where it can be, a `chart::Cover` where it can't.** A cover is a bit a place, set where the garment is; what's drawn reads how far inside the cover's edge a place is (`Cover::edges`, worked out once when a program loads; `chart::inside`, read between places), and the pixel is discarded where that's under 0. Read as marks, an edge was a row of teeth a place wide. But a cover's edge is only as good as what it was written from, and two pieces' covers don't meet: Hakuro's shoulder's rim and his jacket's armhole were two ragged edges with a gap between. His coat's edges are rules of where a point is on him at rest, the same rule for every piece it crosses: its front (`hakuro::opening`: one line from the collar's top to the tails' edges), its armholes (`armhole`: where the coat meets an oval tube about his arm's line, through the body and the shoulder alike), its collar's top (`collar_top`), and where the jacket stops and the tails start (`COAT_SEAM`). A rule's edge is exact at every pixel, and gold binding is drawn along it by the same number. Only the jacket's neck and the top of its body are still its cover's.
- **Where a surface runs nearly along the rule's own surface, cut it on its chart instead.** The armhole's tube runs nearly along a shoulder's side, and cut by the tube the cap's edge was a row of steps. The cap ends in an arch drawn on its own chart (`armhole_edge`): the angle round its path it reaches narrows to nothing just past his shoulder's joint. A binding's width is measured across the cloth, not by distance from the rule's surface, for the same reason.
- **An open sheet is drawn from both faces** (a collar's inside shows from the front). Which faces a draw keeps is fixed when a program is built, so a program writes both draws and chooses between them.
- **Paint** is by place on the chart, or by where the place is on the creature at rest. A garment's pieces take the garment's own paint: painted by where they were on his body, a shoulder's rim out over his arm took his arm's skin colour ("a floating orange crescent").
- **A cloth is its colour and its relief** (`hakuro::Cloth::relief`, m off the form): a seam's welt and its stitches, a rib, a pocket's flap, a weave's thread. The pixel tilts its light by the relief's slope across the screen (`engine::character::embossed`), so what's too fine to be drawn as a form's side is still seen by how it catches light. Each of the programs that draw him asks one function what a pixel wears (`hakuro::dressed`), so his clothes are the same in all of them. A pattern is drawn only where a repeat of it covers eight pixels or more: down to two, a weave's threads beat against the pixels in rings across his chest. A two-faced piece's inside is its lining, shaded away from the edges it shows at: lit all over, what showed of it through an armhole was a bright patch.
- **What's worn under shows through every opening, so paint all of it.** His trunk was his bare shoulder's skin inside the left armhole, and showed through it as a skin-coloured hole in his coat's side when his arm swung back. His trunk is his top all over now.

## Cloth over a charted form

Cloth that hangs off a body (Hakuro's coat's tails: a grid of nodes stepped each frame, `content/hakuro/arms.wrela`) is kept off the body's forms as they are, not off capsules standing in for them. A trouser leg is 8 cm deep one way and 15 the other. A capsule fat enough for the one let the cloth lie inside the other, with an allowance for how far in it hung at rest, and his thigh came through his coat's skirt wherever he stood with a leg out.

- **Read the form once, into a table.** Each leg's radius about each of its straight lengths at rest (thigh, shin), 12 places along by 32 round, written when the program loads (`leg_radii`). A node is taken back to the body at rest by the length's bone, and kept 12 mm clear of the radius there.
- **Don't read the form itself at every node.** `Limb::toward` for each node, each of 16 passes, kept the cloth off the leg as well and took the lab's frame from 13 ms to 103 (with its place searched for along the path, minutes for a clip). With the table the frame is what it was.
- **Cloth pinned to the body is kept only just clear** (3 mm): kept as clear as the cloth that hangs free, its top stood out past the jacket's hem as a ledge.
- **Hold the cloth only along its seam.** The tails were held a hand's width down, from when their top was tucked under a longer jacket; a thigh lifted in a stride came through the held cloth at his hip, and critics chose the old drawing four to nothing on every moving shot. Held over their top 7 cm, they ride up over it.
- **Where a garment's charted part stops, its cloth starts at the same surface.** The jacket stops where the tails start (no hem between: tucked under a jacket that came 6 cm down over them, the two front edges never met), and the tails' girth at their top is the jacket's own outside there, read off its form, through a smooth curve (straight from one number to the next, their top was 24 flat faces). Their folds come in over 30 cm: starting all at once, their start was a ridge that caught the light as a second belt.
- **Keep an edge off what moves under it.** Each front edge hung straight down the middle of a thigh, and a lifted thigh took the edge on its front as a ragged line. The coat is a cutaway now (`shapes::front_edge`): its front edges are cut back from his waist to its tails, fastest at the waist, so by a hand's breadth down the edge is past the front of his thigh.
- **A seam's top edge goes where the garment over it goes.** The tails' top row is the jacket's hem carried on. Put out of a lifted thigh like the cloth under it, while the hem above stayed on his trunk, its front corner stood off the hem as a wing at his belt. Its top 2.5 cm is left where his hips hold it (`sewn`): the corner goes into the fold of his hip with the hem, and the cloth comes out over his thigh under it.
- **Give the cloth a shape as long as the cloth.** The shape the tails are drawn toward was carried a share of the way to where a thigh would take it (a blend of the hips' and the thigh's places, as skinning blends them). That shape is nearer the thigh's joint than the cloth is long: with the thigh square to his hips, a sixth nearer. The cloth gathered to lie on it, and its front edge ruffled at every high step. The shape is turned a share of the thigh's turn about its joint instead (`tails_carried`), which keeps every length.
- **Only what's cloth is kept off him.** The grid runs on in front of the tails' edges where they're cut back; its nodes there go where their neighbours take them (`woven`).
- **Tell the cloth what air it's going through.** A move played in place, or a body carried by what it stands on, gives the cloth no wind of its own. Told how fast he's going (`Drape::work`'s `going`), the cloth hangs back from plumb by the air's push, which grows as his speed squared (`STREAM`): 25° at a run's 4 m/s, 3° at a walk. Hanging straight down at a run, his coat's tails only swung and never streamed. Standing, he's told a light air that swells and ebbs (`manner::air_at`), so his coat stirs while he's still; his hair is pushed back by the same air (`hakuro::hair_blown`).

## How a character moves as itself

A character's moves are captures of other people (`content/moves`); what makes them its own is laid over them, and none of it moves a foot from where a move planted it.

- **A bearing** (`engine::bearing`): its head held steady and its chin down, its chest up, its trunk leant the way it's going with the lean spread up its spine (one hinge at the waist folds it there), its hips tipped and lowered with each leg bent to put its ankle back where it was, and its arms given up to trail behind it. A share of each, so it eases in and out (`Bearing::part`). Hakuro's changes with his speed (`manner::bearing_at`): none standing, his own at a walk, a sprinter's past it.
- **A sprint is the run's stride at a quicker cadence** (`manner::sprint`): the same feet on the same ground in less time, so they meet it as fast as it goes under them, with the bearing a sprint has. Nothing new is captured.
- **A stand is a pose that's kept alive** (`engine::pose`): the pose a look was locked in, written as the list of turns it was set with (a bone and what hangs from it, about one of the body's axes, from the hips out), each turn free to swing a little some whole number of times a loop. Hakuro's (`manner::STAND`) breathes twice in eight seconds and shifts its weight and its look once; `engine::cleanup` stands it on the floor.
- **The hands are the character's** (`manner::palette_of`): a capture holds no fingers of his. Loose going, his left's round over his hip as far as he's standing, both closed as far as he's fighting.

## What hangs off a body

A mass held to a point of a body on a spring (`engine::sway`): it lags when the point speeds up, overshoots when it stops, and settles. It answers the point's change of speed, not its speed, so a runner or a rider on a boat holds it still. Hakuro's hair is on a bone of its own, his head's turned about the middle of his skull by how far the mass has swung (`hakuro::with_hair`): three swings a second, at most 3 cm (further, and its spikes passed through his mask's ears). Every palette he's drawn with goes through `with_hair`. In the lab his hair is stepped on the clock his cloth is, and holds through a hit-stop with him.

## When a species' limb changes

A character's limb is the species' plus a difference, so fitting the species' limb again would move the character's. The tool keeps the character's limb where it is (`kept_over`): forms about one path add, so the new difference is the old one plus the old species' limb less the new, and no reference is read. Hakuro's arms are kept so; the sampled grids they were first fitted to are gone, and `studio/forms/measured.wrela` holds 416 of each grid's radii to measure the arms against. His hands' forms are kept as written for the same reason (their sampled hands are gone), with 208 radii a piece; so are his trouser leg, his trunk, his neck, his head and his jacket's four forms (208 radii each, 504 for the two about his spine). No part of him has a sampled reference in the repository any more.

## Judging a change

`wrela tool judge -- build/judge/<name> --against <revision>` renders the workspace and another revision on a list of shots and writes each critic a directory of pairs, marked A and B, with a brief; `--tally` reads their answers through the sealed key. Each shot is shown each way round to half the panel, because critics lean toward a letter. Read what critics say of each side as well as how they vote: the descriptions repeat from panel to panel when the votes don't.

A round is run in stages, to keep its cost down:

- **Four critics first**, on the shots where the change can show (`--critics 4 --only <shot> ...`). A clear result ends the round: the jacket's was 6 votes to 25. Four more only when the first four are close, and a result still close is reported as close: five critics once gave the same pictures 30 of 30 and then 20 of 30.
- **Critics on a small agent.** A critic needs to read a brief and its pictures and nothing else. On an agent with four tools one used 42 thousand tokens for eleven pictures; on a general-purpose agent, 92 thousand. The pictures are about a quarter of that.
- **Which model.** Over four rounds Opus critics leaned the way Fable critics did every time, at three quarters of the tokens, though they answer "no difference" about half the time. Sonnet critics were at or under a coin in two of the four. Haiku critics leaned the right way on a round whose answer was known (17 to 12, where Fable gave 27 to 15) but named parts that were the same in both pictures.

## What a chart is poor at

- Surfaces square to the path: a shoulder's top, a collar, an end cap. The radius must change steeply there, and the splines round it off. Hakuro's jacket misses most at its collar and shoulders.
- Anything not star-shaped about its path: fingers held together, an ear. Those stay implicit fields.
- A pose far past what the joints' changes were solved over: the changes hold there, and nothing says the held shape is right.
- Where a limb leaves a garment. The limb's root is drawn under the garment (`Part::sunk`), and from behind a raised arm the garment's armhole stands off the arm as a thin line.
- A stiff garment where it's worn over a joint, until some of it is said to stay (`chart::Stay`) and its edges are rules. Hakuro's charted jacket was judged against his sampled one on eleven shots: 6 votes to 25 at first, 23 to 20 as it landed, losing four to nothing where the front of a shoulder was carried round with the arm ("a shoulder flap flaring out") and where the collar's foot read as a panel from behind. Both are gone: the first with the share that stays and the armhole's rule, the second with `Grown::beyond`. Tried for the first before that and taken out, none removing it: a shoulder that goes only part of the way with the arm, a shoulder that comes in as the arm lowers, the panels given to the body's form (a ledge across the back), and a shoulder parted into a cap on the arm and panels on the collarbone (a spike at the collar).
- A form made each frame. A part's forms are constants. A form fitted from a function where the parts are made (`chart::of` inside `charts()`) is a least-squares fit a frame: the combat lab went from 11 ms a frame to 81 until the collar's flare was a constant.
